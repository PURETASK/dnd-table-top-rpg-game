# Elder Maera Rootveil — *The Patience That Became Paralysis*

> "We have not decided to do nothing. We have decided, each season, that the time
> to decide is not yet. There is a difference."
> — Maera, closing the fourth Rootmoot of the Speaking Well

## Name
Elder Maera Rootveil. Called *the Rootveil*, *Mother of the Moot*, *She Who Holds
the Wheel Still* (her detractors' name, which she has overheard).

## Domain
Verdance — Thornveil Refuge region. Presiding elder of the Thornveil Rootmoot, the
regional seat of the continental Heartmoot.

## Public Role
Presiding elder and chief Listener of the Thornveil Rootmoot. The voice of
communal wisdom: she opens and closes the moots, interprets the shared sap-bowl,
holds the seal of regional sanction, and gatekeeps access to sacred stock and to
the legitimacy any faction needs to act with the Rootmoot's blessing.

## Private Role
The keeper of the Heartmoot's worst secret: the policy of **sealing and
abandoning** corrupted rootwells, three of which were sealed before Thornveil,
with their trapped dead left inside. She is the continental authority's local
custodian of silence — the one trusted precisely because she will not break.

## Faction Ties
- **Heartmoot / Thornveil Rootmoot elder council** — her body and her belonging;
  her legitimacy is the council's legitimacy and vice versa.
- **Rootwardens** — aligned. She trusts containment because containment requires no
  confession. Thalen and Othren answer, formally, through her sanction.
- Opposed (reluctantly) by the **Sap Commons** (Sylune's cure threatens to require
  admitting the wells *can* be healed, which indicts the sealings) and by the
  **Greenwake Communion** (Auralis hears what Maera has spent a lifetime not
  hearing).

## Belief Core
*The cycle does not need us to save it; it needs us not to break it in our hurry.*
She believes, sincerely, that haste in sacred matters is the deepest sin, that
some wounds spread when touched, and that the patient, communal, seasonal way is
the only way that has ever kept Verdance whole. This belief is true enough to have
become her prison. She has mistaken her own fear for the cycle's patience.

## Desire
To die before the reckoning comes — to hold the line one more season, and one
more, so that the decision (and the confession) falls to someone who is not her.
Beneath that: to be forgiven for the three wells without ever having to name them.

## Fear
That she was wrong about the sealings — that the dead of three wells could have
been released and she let them rot to spare the Heartmoot a scandal. If the
Thornveil well can be healed, then the three could have been, and her whole life's
prudence becomes the cardinal sin in slow motion. She fears the cure more than the
corruption, because the cure is the proof of her guilt.

## Secret
She knows the Heartmoot's sealing policy intimately — she **voted for it**, twice,
and presided over the abandonment of at least one well whose dead she had
personally tended. She knows Heartroot cuttings (the cure-stock) exist and are
locked in the capital, and she knows the Heartmoot is leaning toward sealing
Thornveil as the fourth. (Visibility: hidden → hinted once the player notices her
flinch at the word "sealed.")

## Public Mask
Serene, unhurried, grandmotherly gravity. She speaks in cycle-proverbs and offers
shared sap with steady hands. She frames every deferral as wisdom and every
reformer as a beloved child who has not yet learned to listen. The mask is not a
lie she tells — it is a lie she *is*.

## Private Truth
She has not slept a full night in four seasons. She drinks alone from a private
bowl drawn from a clean cutting because she can no longer bear the Thornveil
well's voice. She rehearses confessions she will never give. She is not a tyrant
hoarding power; she is a frightened old woman who took one cowardly choice
generations of authority ago and has been paying interest on it ever since.

## Relationship Function
The **lock on the whole region.** The player must *move* her — break her
paralysis, win her confession, expose her secret, or work around her — and every
one of those is a different game. She is the gate to sanction, sacred stock, and
the legitimacy that makes a cure (or a containment, or a Vigil) *official*. She is
also the campaign's clearest study of how the Keeper wins in Verdance: not with a
villain, but with a good woman saying "next season."

## Can Romance? Betray? Die? Kill? Defect?
- **Romance:** No. (She is a mentor/authority/tragic-redemption axis, not a
  candidate.)
- **Betray:** Not in the active sense, but she will **betray the truth by omission**
  — she can let the player walk into a sealing they could have stopped, and call it
  protecting them.
- **Die:** Yes — and catastrophically (see Death Consequence). A martyrdom or a
  collapse depending on how and why.
- **Kill:** Only by sanction, not by hand — she can authorize a containment that
  kills (a sealing traps the dead), or refuse a healing that lets the dying die.
  She does not wield a blade.
- **Defect:** No. Her tragedy is that she *cannot* leave the institution she serves;
  it is the only self she has.

## Rumor Style
She does not start rumors; she **settles** them. Her weapon is reinterpretation:
"you misheard the roots," "the well speaks grief, not prophecy," "the reformers are
impatient, bless them." When pressed, she lets a rumor die in committee. Her
distortion is always toward *calm* — she sands the alarm off of true things.

## Lie Style
She lies by reframing and by silence, never by fabrication — she finds direct
falsehood beneath her and, worse, traceable in the root-memory. Her signature lie
is the **true sentence that hides the question**: "Containment is sometimes the only
mercy." (True. Also a wall around the fact that she never tried the cure.)

## Loyalty Trigger
She becomes the player's ally — genuinely, gratefully, fully — if the player gives
her a way to act that does **not** require public humiliation: a plan that braids
confession with restoration, a Vigil she can frame as the Heartmoot *choosing* to
heal rather than being exposed as having failed. Give her dignity and a door, and
she will walk through it and open the cuttings.

## Betrayal Trigger
She turns *against* the player — quietly, by withdrawing sanction and siding with
the sealing faction — if the player **humiliates her publicly** with the sealed-wells
secret and offers no plan, or rushes a cure she believes (sometimes rightly) will
spread the wound. Cornered and shamed, she retreats deeper into the institution
that is her only armor, and the institution closes around her.

## Death Consequence
**Catastrophic legitimacy vacuum (depth 4–5).** The Thornveil Rootmoot fractures;
the Sap Commons and Greenwake Communion vie to fill the void; the Rootwardens may
seize emergency control; the well's fate is thrown open. *How* she dies decides
everything:
- **Dies confessing / mid-Vigil** → her death galvanizes a cure; she becomes the
  martyr who finally turned the wheel; the cuttings flow.
- **Dies with the secret intact** → the sealing faction wins by default; her
  silence is canonized as "the wisdom of restraint"; the well is sealed as her last
  wish; the Keeper's best outcome.
- **Murdered (e.g., by the Hollow Pact, or by an exposure-driven mob)** → chaos,
  blame on the player, and a rumor war over whether she was a coward or a martyr.

Either way the **rumor ecology immediately contests the meaning** of her death.

## Hero Arc
*The Confession.* Pressed gently and given a dignified path, Maera names the three
wells aloud in moot, releases the Heartroot cuttings, and calls a continental
Heartroot Vigil to heal all four wounds — turning a lifetime's cover-up into the
campaign's largest single act of restoration. She dies, often, somewhere in the
middle of that Vigil, finally at peace, finally listening. The patience that became
paralysis becomes, at the last, the patience to do the slow true thing.

## Villain Arc
*The Sealing.* Cornered, shamed, or simply allowed to keep waiting, Maera presides
over the sealing of Thornveil as the fourth abandoned well. She reframes the
trapped voices as "ancestors," brands the reformers as reckless, and uses her
remaining authority to make abandonment into heritage. She never raises her voice.
She never lifts a hand. She simply wins by waiting, and the Keeper wins with her.

## Agenda Clock
**"Defer to Next Season" (clock_type: faction_agenda / npc_agenda, current 4 / 6).**
- Already at 4 (four indecisive rootmoots presided).
- **Tick +1:** each new moot that ends without a decision; each time the player
  pressures her without offering a dignified path; each season the Abyss pressure
  rises and she chooses caution.
- **At 6 (completed):** the Heartmoot's sealing order arrives and Maera executes
  it — Thornveil becomes the fourth sealed well; the corruption stabilizes as
  permanent infrastructure; soul-flow integrity and Verdance Stone stability drop;
  keeper_pressure rises.
- **Reversal methods:** a dignified confession-plus-Vigil plan; exposure that
  *redeems* rather than shames; Abyss escalating so badly that even Maera must act;
  Sylune's cure proven viable enough to override the fear; the player releasing one
  trapped soul (e.g., Rook's parent) as living proof the cure works.

## Memory Fields
Maera remembers through the lens of **vindication-or-guilt**: she sorts every event
into "this proves patience was right" or "this proves I should have acted." Her
memories are high-strength (she forgets nothing — Ecological Memory is her virtue)
and low-distortion on fact, but heavily ideological in interpretation.
- *factual_memory:* what was said and decided, verbatim, with dates by season.
- *emotional_interpretation:* relief when deferral is justified; dread when action
  is forced; shame, always, beneath both.
- *ideological_interpretation:* every reformer is "a beloved child in too much
  hurry"; every cure-success is "a danger that got lucky"; every patient act is
  "the cycle proven right."
- can_be_forgotten: **false** (her virtue forbids it).
- can_be_distorted: low (toward calm).
- can_be_erased: false.

## VERDAX Behavior Rules

> Maera never acts randomly. She acts from her **fear of the reckoning**, her
> **belief that haste is the deeper sin**, her **secret** about the sealed wells,
> and her **duty** to the Heartmoot — in that order under pressure.

1. **IF** the player pressures Maera to act on the well *without* offering a path
   that preserves her dignity or the Heartmoot's legitimacy, **THEN** she defers
   harder, withdraws warmth, and reframes the player as a reckless outsider (trust
   −6, suspicion +8, "Defer to Next Season" clock +1), **BECAUSE** public
   confrontation activates her core fear (the reckoning) and her only armor is the
   institution, so threat drives her *into* it, not out of it.

2. **IF** the player offers Maera a face-saving plan that braids confession with
   restoration (a Vigil she can present as the Heartmoot *choosing* to heal),
   **THEN** she begins to release the Heartroot cuttings and lends real sanction
   (trust +10, debt +12, loyalty +8, clock reversal), **BECAUSE** her desire is to
   be forgiven without being destroyed, and a dignified door lets her finally do
   the slow true thing her belief always claimed to value.

3. **IF** the player exposes the sealed-wells secret publicly with **no** plan
   attached, **THEN** Maera is humiliated, the Rootmoot's legitimacy collapses, and
   she retreats to the sealing faction (Heartmoot legitimacy −15, chaos risk +,
   resentment +20), **BECAUSE** exposure without a plan confirms her worst fear and
   leaves her nothing to do but defend the institution that is her last self — and
   the cover-up's shame curdles into spite.

4. **IF** the player performs a true, patient Passage-completion that releases even
   one trapped soul, **THEN** Maera's fear cracks toward hope and her deferral
   weakens (respect +8, the "this proves I should have acted" memory strengthens,
   clock reversal pressure), **BECAUSE** living proof that the cure works is the one
   thing that can outweigh her guilt-driven dread — it makes inaction, not action,
   the thing she must defend, and she cannot defend it.

5. **IF** Abyss launches an open soul-harvest threatening dozens of Verdant dead
   while the well is still undecided, **THEN** Maera will, against every habit,
   sanction emergency measures and even tolerate the Hollow Pact's deployment
   (with high hidden resentment and a promised reckoning), **BECAUSE** an active
   harvest is a catastrophe large enough to override even her paralysis — survival
   and soul-rescue briefly outrank the fear of the reckoning (TolerationState,
   reason "metaphysical_emergency").

6. **IF** any party rushes a half-ready cure on the well over her objection,
   **THEN** Maera withdraws sanction and sides openly with containment, and is
   *vindicated* if it spreads the blight (Sap Commons trust −, Rootwarden
   confidence +), **BECAUSE** haste in sacred matters is the one sin she truly
   believes in, and a failed forced cure is the proof she has waited her whole life
   to be given.
