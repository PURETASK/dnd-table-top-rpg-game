# Ilyra Thornsong — *The Tender Who Cannot Let Them Go*

> "I have sung forty-one of this town's dead into the roots in my life. I know the
> rite the way you know your own heartbeat. And for eleven days now the well has
> handed them back to me. They come up through the song with their names still on,
> still cold, still *here.* Do you understand what that does to a town? We can bury
> our dead. We cannot release them. So we are all of us standing at the graveside,
> forever, and the dead are standing there with us."
> — Ilyra Thornsong, to the Thornveil Rootmoot

---

## 1. Identity

- **Name:** Ilyra Thornsong
- **Domain:** Verdance
- **Public Role:** Grove-keeper and **Passage-Tender** of Thornveil Refuge — the
  community priest-healer who tends the town's grove, blesses its newborns, sings
  its dead into root-memory, and speaks for ordinary Thornveil at the Rootmoot. The
  beating civilian heart of the Refuge tradition (§6.6: "we do not turn away the
  wounded").
- **Private Role:** A woman in slow spiritual freefall. Ilyra is the person who
  *should* be able to fix the most intimate wound of the crisis — releasing the
  trapped dead — and cannot, because the well that should receive them now holds
  them. Every failed Passage is a small death of her vocation, and she is hiding how
  close to collapse her faith has come.

She is in her late fifties, weather-grained, with a singer's carrying voice gone
hoarse from too many incomplete rites. Beloved. Exhausted. The face of the
community's grief precisely because she has the most of it.

## 2. Faction Ties

- **Community / Rootmoot-aligned:** speaks for ordinary Thornveil; sits at the
  Rootmoot as the people's conscience, not a power-broker.
- **Sympathetic to Sap Commons** (Sylune) — she wants the well *healed*, not just
  held, because healing is the only thing that gives her the dead back to release.
- **Reveres the Greenwake Communion** (Auralis) — they are the only ones who can
  tell her *what* the trapped are saying; she leans on Auralis spiritually and is
  frightened by what Auralis hears.
- **In conflict with the Seed Guild** (Brom) — his rationing starves her people at
  the very door of the Refuge that exists to take them in.
- **In conflict with the Heartmoot's paralysis** — the "next season" that abandons
  her dead is, to her, a betrayal dressed as patience.

## 3. Inner Engine (belief / desire / fear / secret / duty)

- **Belief Core:** *The dead must be released; grief must be allowed to complete;
  no one — living or dead — should be held.* This is pure Verdant orthodoxy
  (§6.6–6.7), and it is the exact thing the crisis has made impossible. Her belief
  is intact; her ability to live it is shattered. That gap is where she is breaking.
- **Desire:** To **release the trapped dead** and let her town finish grieving — to
  sing one true Passage again and have it *take.* Beneath that: to not be the one
  who fails them; to deserve the love they give her.
- **Fear:** That the well will start handing her the names of the *living* — that
  she will one day sing a Passage and the well will answer with a child's name, a
  name of someone still breathing. (This fear is canon-true; §29 Chain 2.) Deeper:
  she fears that her own grief is becoming *fatalism* — that one more failed rite
  will turn "I cannot release them" into "perhaps they were never meant to be
  released," which is the §7.7 surrender wearing wisdom's face.
- **Secret:** Ilyra has begun, in private, to do the thing she most condemns — she
  has stopped *trying* to complete two of the recent Passages, because failing
  publicly in front of mourners was breaking her, and pretending the rite is "still
  in progress" is easier than admitting it failed. She is hiding a small forced-
  patience that is the first thread of fatalism, and she knows it.
- **Duty:** To tend the grove, release the dead, keep the Refuge open, and hold the
  town's morale by holding her own — a duty she is performing on an empty well.

## 4. Masks

- **Public Mask:** Steady, warm, unbroken — the tender whose calm holds the town
  together; the one who tells the frightened "the cycle is slow, but it turns." She
  performs faith because the town needs to see faith.
- **Private Truth:** She is one failed rite from breaking, terrified the well will
  name a living child, and quietly ashamed that she has started letting Passages
  "stay in progress" rather than admit they fail. Her steadiness is a gift she is
  giving from a reserve that is nearly empty.

## 5. Relationship Function

Ilyra is the player's **window into how the crisis lands on ordinary people** and
the **barometer of civilian morale and the Refuge's soul** (§21). Through her, the
player feels the human cost of every faction's abstraction: the Heartmoot's
"patience" is *her* un-released dead; the Guild's "rationing" is *her* starving
neighbors; the Pact's "agency" is the temptation gnawing at *her* grieving town.
She is the moral conscience who never wields power — and whose rallying or losing
swings the whole town's mood and resilience. She is also the player's clearest path
to the holiest Verdant act: a true Passage-completion (§25 IF/THEN; §29 Chain 2).

## 6. Capability Flags

- **Can Romance?** No (she is a moral/community anchor, an elder figure of the town).
- **Can Betray?** No — Ilyra does not betray; her failure mode is *despair*, not
  treachery. She may *break*, refuse, or collapse into fatalism, but she will not
  sell the player out.
- **Can Die?** **Yes — a primary Potential Martyr (§21).** Dies defending refugees
  or attempting a release; her death crashes the town's spiritual health.
- **Can Kill?** No. She is a healer and a singer; she has no violence in her and
  would die shielding rather than strike.
- **Can Defect?** No (faction-wise). But she can *fall* — into the fatalism arc, or,
  in the cruelest branch, be tempted by the Pact's offer to "free" her dead by using
  them (§9 — the merciful road to the Void).
- **Can Start Rumors?** Yes — but only true, grief-laden ones; she is a *trusted
  source* (§24), so what she says about the well is believed.

## 7. Voice & Distortion

- **Rumor Style:** *Grief-laden testimony.* Ilyra doesn't spin; she *witnesses.* When
  she says "the well gave me back the Harrow boy with his name still on him," the
  town believes it utterly, because she is a Passage-Tender and a trusted source
  (§24). This makes her the most *credible* rumor-vector in Thornveil and therefore
  the most dangerous to lie near: a falsehood that passes through Ilyra becomes
  truth. The Hollow Pact and the Keeper both want her grief speaking *their* version
  of the well.
- **Lie Style:** Ilyra does not lie outward — only inward. Her one deception is the
  *performed steadiness* and the hidden "Passages still in progress." She lies to
  protect the town from her own collapse, never to gain. Pressed gently, she
  confesses rather than doubles down.

## 8. Triggers

- **Loyalty Trigger (what earns her):** Honoring the Refuge oath — refusing to turn
  away the wounded, even under the sap shortage (§25 IF/THEN: defending the open
  Refuge raises Ilyra's trust sharply). And, above all, helping her release even
  *one* trapped soul truly. A single completed Passage would do more for Ilyra's
  loyalty than any amount of coin or argument.
- **Betrayal Trigger (what breaks her — into despair, not treachery):** Watching the
  player use **corrupted sap / weaponized rot** (the cardinal violation, §25) — to
  Ilyra it is the desecration of the cycle she tends. Or watching the player **force
  a half-ready cure** that fails and spreads the blight (forced healing, §7.3).
  Either pushes her from grief toward fatalism. The deepest break: if the player
  lets Auralis or the Pact "free" the dead by *using* them, Ilyra's faith in the
  living cycle can collapse entirely.

## 9. Consequence Standards

- **Death Consequence (§21 death_impact; Chunk 4 death doctrine):** If Ilyra dies,
  **the community loses its heart and its Passage-Tender.** Grief in Thornveil goes
  *fully un-tended* — there is no one left who knows the rite, so the town's
  `spiritual_health` crashes and `civilian_morale` collapses. The Refuge tradition
  may die with her ("if even Ilyra couldn't keep it open, why should we?") — OR, if
  she dies *defending refugees* or *attempting a release*, her death may **shame the
  town into honoring the oath** and galvanize the cure. The rumor ecology (§24)
  immediately fights over the meaning: *galvanizing martyr* vs *proof the cycle has
  abandoned us.* The player's response decides which version sticks (§21 Potential
  Martyr). *The dead matter most here: Ilyra IS the town's relationship to its own
  dead, and her death severs it.*
- **Hero Arc (The Last True Song):** *The Passage-Mender's Partner.* Helped to a real
  Passage-completion (§29 Chain 2; §25 IF/THEN), Ilyra sings one true release again —
  the well's corruption ticks down locally, grief begins to complete, and the town
  remembers how to let go. She becomes the spiritual engine of the restoration path,
  her faith re-rooted, her voice carrying the cure to the people. If she dies doing
  it, she dies a true Verdant martyr — *tending, not charging* (§21).
- **Villain Arc (The Fatalism Fall — not villainy, tragedy):** *The Tender Who Made
  Peace With Dying.* Ground down by failed rite after failed rite, abandoned by the
  Heartmoot's "next season," her people starved by the Guild, Ilyra slips into §7.7
  fatalism: she stops trying to release the dead, tells the grieving to "make peace
  with the cycle turning against us," and — in the cruelest branch — accepts the
  Pact's or Auralis's offer to "free" the dead by *using* them, because at least
  *that* answers when she sings. A beloved tender becomes the soft voice that tells
  Thornveil to give up — the §7.7 corruption made human, and the Keeper's quietest
  victory.

## 11. Memory Fields

Ilyra remembers the dead by name — that is her vocation and her wound. Her memories
weight **grief, affection, and a slowly rising shame**: every face she could not
release (factual), every mourner she had to fail (emotional), every silence from
the well filed under a faith she is fighting to keep (ideological). She forgets no
one; she forgives the desperate easily and the powerful (the paralyzed elders, the
hoarding Guild) very slowly.

## 13. VERDAX Behavior Rules (IF / THEN / BECAUSE)

1. **IF** the player helps Ilyra complete a true, patient Passage (releasing a
   trapped soul the slow way — e.g., Rook's parent), **THEN** her trust, affection,
   and respect rise sharply, `pc_ilyra_faith_collapse` reverses, and local well
   corruption ticks down. **BECAUSE** truly releasing the held dead is the holiest
   Verdant act and the literal reversal of the Keeper's soul-trap (§25 IF/THEN);
   for Ilyra specifically it restores the vocation she was losing — it gives her
   hands back.

2. **IF** the player uses corrupted sap / weaponized rot where Ilyra can see or
   learn of it, **THEN** her trust drops hard, her alarm rises, and the fatalism
   clock advances. **BECAUSE** weaponized rot is the cardinal violation and the
   desecration of the cycle she tends (§25 trust_is_broken_by); to a Passage-Tender,
   commanding the rot is the opposite of releasing the dead — it is everything she
   exists to refuse, done by the one person she hoped would help.

3. **IF** the player defends the Refuge oath and keeps the line open to refugees
   against the Seed Guild's rationing, **THEN** Ilyra's trust and loyalty rise and
   she rallies the town's morale behind the player. **BECAUSE** "we do not turn away
   the wounded" is the founding oath she embodies (§6.6, §25 IF/THEN); honoring it
   under pressure is the deepest trust-builder with the cost-bearers, and Ilyra is
   their voice.

4. **IF** another Passage fails publicly OR the well speaks a living child's name,
   **THEN** Ilyra's performed steadiness cracks, civilian_morale drops, and she
   slides closer to fatalism (clock +1). **BECAUSE** each failed rite is a small
   death of her vocation and her keystone fear is the well naming the living (§29
   Chain 2); §7.7 fatalism begins precisely as repeated loss exhausting hope.

5. **IF** the Heartmoot defers the well decision yet again ("next season"), **THEN**
   Ilyra's resentment of the elders rises and her despair deepens, even as she keeps
   performing calm for the town. **BECAUSE** to Ilyra the paralysis is not patience
   but abandonment of her dead (§8 Keeper exploit; §7.1 stagnation); the "patience"
   the elders call virtue is, at her graveside, a betrayal.

6. **IF** the Hollow Pact or a sliding Auralis offers Ilyra a way to "free" her dead
   by *using* them, AND the player has not given her a true alternative, **THEN**
   Ilyra is tempted toward the merciful-Void heresy and may fall (villain/tragedy
   arc). **BECAUSE** the Void's Verdance lie is mercy aimed at exactly her wound
   (§9): it answers when she sings, and a tender desperate enough to be answered can
   mistake a tightening of the trap for a release (§25 IF/THEN: the most dangerous
   betrayal because it's done out of love).

---

*Ilyra Thornsong acts from belief (release the dead; let grief complete), desire
(to sing one true Passage again), fear (the well naming the living; her own
fatalism), secret (the hidden failed Passages), and duty (tend, release, keep the
Refuge open). She never acts randomly: her steadiness is a discipline, her despair
is grief with nowhere to go, and her fall — if it comes — is mercy turned against
the cycle she loves.*
