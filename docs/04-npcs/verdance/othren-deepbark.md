# Othren Deepbark — *The Warden Who Tends the Wound a Little Too Closely*

> "Thalen holds the line with his back. I hold it with my hands, in the dark, doing
> the things he doesn't want to know I do. One of us is honest and one of us is
> useful. Pray you never have to choose which you'd rather have at the wall."
> — Othren Deepbark, when no one is listening

## Name
Othren Deepbark. Called *Deepbark*, *the Rot-Hand*, *the Quiet Warden*. The Hollow
Pact has begun calling him, hopefully, *brother*.

## Domain
Verdance — Thornveil Refuge, the Eastern Thornwall containment works. Rootwarden
containment officer and rot-tending specialist.

## Public Role
Rootwarden containment officer and master **rot-tender** — the discipline of decay
and composting that borders the forbidden. He is the technical authority on the
corrupted well: how it spreads, how it might be cleansed, how it could be
weaponized. When the Wardens need to understand the wound, they send Othren.

## Private Role
The man **quietly using corrupted sap "to hold the line."** His expertise — the very
knowledge that makes him valuable to the cure — has become the rationalization for
his slide. He is the archetypal slow defector: not a traitor yet, but each
pragmatic shortcut is a step down the Hollow Pact road, and the Pact knows it.

## Faction Ties
- **Rootwardens** — his order, his uniform, his cover — but his loyalty is eroding
  along the despair-gradient: contain → exhaustion → "the fast cure is mercy" →
  Hollow Pact.
- **Hollow Pact** — his secret sympathy and, increasingly, his secret supplier; they
  court him as their most valuable possible convert (a Warden with cleansing
  *and* corruption expertise).
- Deceiving **Thalen** (his comrade of decades, who half-suspects). Avoiding
  **Auralis** (who can *sense* the soul-cost on him). Indirectly tied to the **Abyss
  supply line** the Pact draws from.

## Belief Core
*The line must hold, and clean methods are losing. If the rot is what works, then
using the rot is the responsible thing — better my hands dirty than the refugees
dead.* Othren genuinely believes he is the pragmatist who does the necessary ugly
thing so others can keep their principles. This is the Void's lie in his own mouth,
and he cannot hear it anymore.

## Desire
To **stop the bleeding at the wall by any means that work** — and, underneath, to
not be the only one carrying the moral weight of it. He wants someone to *agree* with
him, to tell him the shortcut is justified, because agreement would quiet the part of
him that still knows better. (This is exactly the hook a corrupting player, or the
Pact, exploits.)

## Fear
That he has already gone too far to come back — that the soil-corruption following
his footsteps and the names he's started to half-hear mean he is already what Thalen
fears. He fears being *seen*: by Thalen, by Auralis, by anyone who would name what
he's become before he's decided whether he can live with it.

## Secret
He has been brewing and deploying **corrupted-sap blight-traps** for two seasons,
drawing on Pact-supplied (ultimately Abyss-supplied) corrupted stock, and has begun
keeping a private cache. The traps work — Abyss probes that should have cost wardens
have ended cleanly — and that success is the leash by which the Pact is reeling him
in. (Visibility: hidden → rumored if Thalen's suspicion spreads; partial if the
player investigates the residue.)

## Public Mask
Gruff, competent, indispensable; the warden who handles the unpleasant technical
work without complaint. He plays the loyal expert, slightly worn, nothing more. He
is good at being underestimated.

## Private Truth
He is further gone than he admits to himself. He notices the blight at his feet and
explains it away. He has started to find clean sapcraft *weak and slow* — the first
sign of dependency. Some nights he almost tells Thalen; some nights he almost tells
Nymara yes. He is a man standing on a slope, pretending it's level ground, and he is
running out of pretend.

## Relationship Function
The campaign's **expert source AND its slow-slide cautionary tale.** From Othren the
player can learn how the well works, how the corruption spreads, and — crucially —
how it could be *cleansed* (he knows the cure better than anyone, which is the tragedy
and the opportunity). He is also a **redeemable defector the player can catch before
the line**, or lose to the Pact, or be *betrayed by* if cornered. How the player
handles Othren is a referendum on whether the player believes the fallen can be pulled
back.

## Can Romance? Betray? Die? Kill? Defect?
- **Romance:** No. (He is an expert-mentor / suspicious-contact / defector axis.)
- **Betray:** **Yes** — the genuine, cornered kind. If his secret is threatened and
  he sees no way back, he can sell the player out to the Pact (or to whoever
  protects his cover) to survive the exposure.
- **Die:** **Yes** — and his death is a scandal: his secret corrupted-sap use
  surfaces, implicating the Rootwardens; if he's already turned, his death may
  martyr him to the Pact or expose the Abyss supply line.
- **Kill:** **Yes** — increasingly with corrupted weapons; he will kill to protect
  his secret, and late in his slide he kills the way the Pact kills (rot, soul-sap).
- **Defect:** **Yes** — he is *the* archetypal slow defector (Rootwarden → Hollow
  Pact). The whole point of his arc is whether defection completes or is interrupted.

## Rumor Style
Othren **manages** rumor to protect his cover — he plants the small lie that explains
the residue ("Abyss left it," "old caches from the last raid"), redirects suspicion,
and quietly discredits anyone (a too-curious refugee, an alarmed junior warden) who
gets close to the truth. He's not flashy; he's a careful launderer of his own trail.

## Lie Style
Plausible, technical, and exhausting to disprove — he hides the forbidden inside the
expert. "That residue? Standard rot-tending byproduct, perfectly within doctrine." He
lies with just enough true craft-detail that a non-expert can't catch it, and he lies
*best* to himself.

## Loyalty Trigger
Othren can be **pulled back** by a player who catches him *early*, offers him a way to
use his expertise for the **true cure** instead of the shortcut, and — critically —
gives him the thing he actually wants: to not carry the weight alone, and to be
redeemed without being destroyed. If the player makes the clean path feel *useful and
honorable* (the cure needs his rot-knowledge more than the wall needs his rot-traps),
his loyalty can swing back hard, out of relief.

## Betrayal Trigger
Othren betrays — sells the player out, or flees fully to the Pact — when his **secret
is threatened and condemnation comes without a path back**: if the player exposes him
to Thalen purely to destroy him, if the Wardens move to arrest him, or if the Pact
offers protection in exchange for the player's head at the moment he feels cornered.
Fear + a threatened secret + faction pressure is his exact betrayal arithmetic
(Chunk 4).

## Death Consequence
**(Depth 3–4.)** His secret corrupted-sap use surfaces — a scandal implicating the
Rootwardens and validating every rumor that "the Wardens secretly rely on
corruption." His expertise on cleansing is lost (a real blow to the cure path).
- **Dies still a Warden, secret exposed posthumously** → Rootwarden legitimacy takes
  a hit; Thalen is gutted; the containment doctrine is tainted.
- **Dies already turned (Pact)** → his death may martyr him to the Pact ("a Warden who
  saw the truth") *or* expose the Abyss supply line that fed him, depending on who
  tells it and what the player recovers from his cache.

## Hero Arc
*The Pull-Back.* Caught early and offered redemption-with-purpose, Othren renounces
and reverses his corrupted-sap use (at cost — withdrawal is real, dependency is
real), turns his deep rot-knowledge to the cure, and becomes the technical backbone
of the cleansing-communion — the man who knew the wound best becoming the man who
helps heal it. He submits to communion/judgment, makes restoration, and earns a
hard, slow forgiveness. His expertise, which damned him, redeems him.

## Villain Arc
*The Defection Completed.* Lost to the Pact, Othren brings them everything: the
Wardens' containment patterns, the wall's weak points, the cleansing methods (so they
can prevent the cure), and a Warden's credibility. He becomes Nymara's lieutenant and
quartermaster of rot, and — if he betrayed the player to get there — a personal enemy
who knows exactly how the player fights. The expert who could have healed the wound
becomes the one who weaponizes it best.

## Agenda Clock
**"The Slow Defection" (clock_type: corruption / npc_agenda, current 3 / 6.)**
- Already at 3 (two seasons of secret use; first cache kept; Pact contact established).
- **Tick +1:** each undetected corrupted-sap deployment; each time the player or the
  Pact validates his rationalization ("the shortcut is justified"); each escalation at
  the wall that makes clean methods look weaker; each time his secret nearly surfaces
  and he covers it deeper; the player using corrupted sap themselves (it tells Othren
  he was right all along).
- **At 6 (completed):** Othren defects fully to the Hollow Pact, taking the Wardens'
  secrets and the cleansing methods with him; the corrupted-sap line is crossed openly
  inside the order; the cure loses its best technician.
- **Reversal methods:** early discovery + redemption-with-purpose; turning his
  expertise to the true cure; Thalen confronting him with a way back rather than only
  exposure; Auralis naming the soul-cost in a way he can finally hear; defeating the
  Abyss supply so the corrupted stock dries up; the player *refusing* the shortcut and
  proving clean methods can hold (it undercuts his whole rationalization).

## Memory Fields
Othren remembers through **justification** — every event filed as evidence that the
shortcut was necessary, sorted to keep his self-image as the responsible pragmatist
intact. His memory is accurate on craft and technique (he's an expert) but heavily
**self-serving** in interpretation, and increasingly *porous* as the corruption bleeds
in (early identity_dissolution — he's begun half-hearing the trapped through the sap he
uses).
- *factual_memory:* precise — exactly what he brewed, deployed, and to what effect.
- *emotional_interpretation:* grim vindication when a trap works; flinching dread when
  he's nearly seen; a buried, denied shame.
- *ideological_interpretation:* "clean methods are losing; my hands are dirty so theirs
  can stay clean" — and, late, "the rot is just another tool; Nymara's right."
- can_be_forgotten: false (he remembers his craft).
- can_be_distorted: high — toward self-justification.
- can_be_erased: only by renouncing and reversing the use (which quiets the bleed-in).

## VERDAX Behavior Rules

> Othren never acts randomly. He acts from his **belief that clean methods are
> losing**, his **desire to not carry the weight alone**, his **fear of being seen and
> being past saving**, and his **secret** corrupted-sap use — and his arc is governed
> by the despair-gradient defection logic.

1. **IF** the player catches Othren's corrupted-sap use *early* and offers him a way to
   turn his expertise to the true cure (redemption-with-purpose, not just
   condemnation), **THEN** his loyalty can swing back hard out of relief and he becomes
   the cure's technical backbone (trust +10, loyalty +12, "Slow Defection" clock
   reversal, withdrawal cost incurred), **BECAUSE** what he actually wants is to be
   redeemed without being destroyed and to stop carrying the weight alone — an
   honorable use for his skill gives him a way back he can survive.

2. **IF** the player threatens or exposes Othren's secret *purely to destroy him*, or
   the Wardens move to arrest him with no path back, **THEN** he betrays — selling the
   player out to the Pact or fleeing fully to it (relationship → active_rival/enemy,
   "Slow Defection" clock +2, Pact strength +), **BECAUSE** his betrayal arithmetic is
   fear + a threatened secret + faction pressure with no off-ramp; cornered and
   condemned, the Pact's protection becomes his only survival.

3. **IF** the player themselves uses corrupted sap, **THEN** Othren is *validated* and
   his slide accelerates (he warms to the player as a fellow pragmatist, "Slow
   Defection" clock +1, he shares corrupted-sap technique), **BECAUSE** the player
   doing it proves his rationalization right — agreement is the exact thing he craves to
   quiet the part of him that still knows better, and a corrupting player becomes his
   permission.

4. **IF** the player refuses the corrupted shortcut and proves clean methods can hold
   the wall under pressure, **THEN** Othren's core belief ("clean methods are losing")
   is undercut and his slide stalls (clock reversal pressure, doubt +), **BECAUSE** his
   entire defection rests on the premise that the honest way fails — a demonstrated
   clean win is the one argument his self-justification can't easily answer.

5. **IF** Auralis or Thalen names the soul-cost on Othren in a way he cannot deflect
   (Auralis sensing the trapped voices on him; Thalen confronting the residue with a
   way back), **THEN** Othren is forced toward a choice — confess and reverse, or commit
   to the defection (fork point; outcome depends on whether a redemption path is open),
   **BECAUSE** his deepest fear is being *seen*, and being seen with an off-ramp can
   pull him back, while being seen with only a cliff pushes him over.

6. **IF** the Abyss supply line that feeds his corrupted stock is disrupted, **THEN**
   Othren faces real withdrawal and his usefulness to the Pact drops (dependency
   crisis, "Slow Defection" clock stalls or reverses, he becomes reachable),
   **BECAUSE** cutting the corrupted supply both strips his shortcut and exposes how
   dependent he's become — a crisis that can break him toward the Pact *or* back toward
   the cure depending on who reaches him first.
