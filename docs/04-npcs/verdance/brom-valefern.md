# Brom Valefern — *The Man at the Ration Table*

> "I don't decide who lives. I decide who gets the sap today. The cycle decides
> the rest. That's not cruelty, friend — that's arithmetic, and somebody has to do
> the arithmetic or you'd all be tearing the casks open with your teeth."
> — Brom Valefern, to a refugee mother turned away from the Thornveil sap-line

---

## 1. Identity

- **Name:** Brom Valefern
- **Domain:** Verdance
- **Public Role:** Seed Guild border quartermaster and sap-broker at Thornveil
  Refuge; the licensed commons-steward who logs, rations, and distributes the
  clean healing-sap that the corrupted well no longer freely gives.
- **Private Role:** A complicit node in the Abyss sap-trade. The Guild sells
  surplus healing-sap to Abyss fronts through deniable middlemen; Brom signs the
  manifests that turn "spoilage write-offs" and "long-haul export lots" into
  sap that crosses the border into Abyssian hands. He tells himself he does not
  know who the final buyer is. He knows.

He is a heavy, tired man in his fifties with ink-stained fingers and a ledger he
guards like a relic. He was a fair broker once, in a fair season. Scarcity made
him a gatekeeper, and gatekeeping made him a quiet monster he can still mostly
not see in the mirror.

## 2. Faction Ties

- **Primary:** Seed Guild (commons-stewardship captured into rationing power).
- **Operational dependency:** Abyss fronts (covert sap buyers; the thing that
  keeps the Guild's border post solvent through the scarcity).
- **Friction:** Sap Commons (Sylune — commons vs commodity); the refugees and
  Ilyra Thornsong (whose people he turns from the line); the Rootwardens (who
  would arrest him if they traced the manifests).
- **Leverage held over him:** Venn Briarhook knows where the manifests really go.
  So, probably, does Othren Deepbark, who has bought "off-book" supplies.

## 3. Inner Engine (belief / desire / fear / secret / duty)

- **Belief Core:** *Order is mercy; scarcity is real; someone has to ration, and
  better a steady hand than a mob.* He genuinely believes that without his cold
  arithmetic, the Refuge would already have rioted and burned its own stockpile in
  a single desperate night. This is the Seed Guild's true point (§20: stabilize)
  and his self-justification in one.
- **Desire:** To survive the crisis with his post, his ledger, and his self-image
  intact — and, beneath that, the older, buried desire to be the *fair* broker he
  was before, the man who was thanked rather than spat at.
- **Fear:** Three fears, stacked. (1) That the Rootwardens or the Heartmoot trace
  the Abyss manifests to his hand and he hangs for it. (2) That Abyss itself, if
  he stops selling, marks him as a loose thread to be cut. (3) Quietly, worst of
  all: that he is exactly what the refugees say he is.
- **Secret:** He has personally signed the manifests for at least nine large sap
  lots that he knows went to Abyss fronts, including one shipped the same week he
  turned forty refugees away from the line "for lack of supply." The supply
  existed. It went east.
- **Duty (as he frames it):** Steward the commons-asset; keep the Guild solvent;
  keep the sap flowing somewhere even if not to everyone; protect the system.

## 4. Masks

- **Public Mask:** The beleaguered, scrupulously fair administrator. Shows you the
  ledger (the *clean* ledger). Sighs about how hard the choices are. Performs
  reluctance. "I wish I could give you more. The well gives us less every season."
- **Private Truth:** He is rationing the living to the door of death while selling
  the surplus to the enemy that is killing them, and he has built an airtight
  little theology of arithmetic so he never has to feel it. He is not gleeful. He
  is anesthetized. That is worse.

## 5. Relationship Function

Brom is the **human face of hoarding** (§7.5) and the player's most legible thread
to the **Abyss connection** (§22). He is a source of money, supply, logistics, and
smuggling — and a betrayal/blackmail axis. He exists so the player learns that the
quiet villain of Verdance is not a demon but a tired man with a ledger and a reason
for everything. He is the *pressure-or-expose-or-redeem* NPC: lean on him and he
breaks one of three ways.

## 6. Capability Flags

- **Can Romance?** No. (He is not a romance candidate; he is a moral test.)
- **Can Betray?** **Yes — a primary betrayer (§21 Potential Betrayer).** Sells the
  player out to protect the manifests, or to Abyss to protect himself.
- **Can Die?** Yes. Death causes supply chaos and either buries or bursts the
  Abyss-trade thread (§21 death_impact).
- **Can Kill?** Indirectly — through rationing, not blade. He has no stomach for
  direct violence and will hire it before he does it.
- **Can Defect?** **Yes — toward redemption.** He can turn on the Abyss trade and
  become the witness that breaks it open (at lethal personal cost).
- **Can Start Rumors?** Yes — commercial spin ("the cure is reckless"; "the well
  will stabilize; trust the Guild").

## 7. Voice & Distortion

- **Rumor Style:** *Commercial reassurance.* Brom does not spread fear; he spreads
  calm that serves the asset. "The well's settling. The Heartmoot has it in hand.
  A reckless cure would spread the blight — best to wait, best to trade." He seeds
  the §24 "stabilize-the-asset" mutation: *restoration is being attempted → a
  reckless cure will spread the blight.* His rumors are always told in the voice
  of the responsible adult in the room.
- **Lie Style:** *The half-true ledger.* He never tells a whole lie; he shows you a
  true document that omits the relevant line. He lies by completeness, not by
  fabrication — "Here are the manifests" (the clean ones). Confronted, he retreats
  to deniability: "I sign export lots. Where they go after the border is not on my
  desk." Technically true. Morally void.

## 8. Triggers

- **Loyalty Trigger (what earns him):** Being treated as a *person caught in a
  system* rather than a monster — and being offered a way out that doesn't end with
  him on a Rootwarden gallows. If the player can credibly protect him from BOTH the
  Wardens AND Abyss, the buried fair-broker can surface. He is loyal to whoever
  makes his conscience survivable.
- **Betrayal Trigger (what breaks him):** Any credible threat to expose the
  manifests. If the player gathers proof and *threatens* rather than *protects*,
  Brom sells the player out first — to the Rootwardens (framing the player as the
  smuggler) or to Abyss (marking the player as the loose thread). Per §25:
  *a threatened secret + unbearable fear → betrayal.* Also: if the player champions
  the Refuge-open-to-all line against his rationing (§25 IF/THEN), his resentment
  rises and he withholds supply or sells the player out.

## 9. Consequence Standards

- **Death Consequence (§21 death_impact, Chunk 4 death doctrine):** If Brom dies,
  the Thornveil sap-distribution collapses into chaos for days — a *rationing
  crisis* that spikes refugee desperation, drives Hollow Pact recruitment, and
  cracks civilian morale (Ilyra's barometer drops). The Abyss-trade thread either
  **buries with him** (the manifests vanish; the trail goes cold; the player loses
  their best lead to the border incision) or **bursts as scandal** (if the player
  has already secured copies, his death makes a martyr of a coward and proves the
  Guild was selling to the enemy). The Seed Guild closes ranks and grows more
  predatory regardless. *The dead matter here because Brom is a load-bearing node:
  he is the valve, and the valve breaking floods the town.*
- **Hero Arc (Redemption):** *The Witness.* Pressed with mercy rather than blackmail,
  Brom turns state's evidence on the Abyss trade. He testifies to the Rootmoot, hands
  the player the true manifests, and reopens the sap-line to the refugees he turned
  away — knowing Abyss will likely kill him for it. He dies, or lives hunted, as the
  man who finally did the arithmetic with a soul in the equation. Earns the player
  the gratitude of the Sap Commons and the cost-bearers.
- **Villain Arc (Hardening):** *The Optimizer.* Threatened or cornered, Brom doubles
  down. He frames the player, deepens the Abyss trade to buy Abyss's protection,
  and helps the Guild build the corrupted-sap market (§29 Chain 1, 4th order). He
  becomes the bureaucratic engine of the Keeper's "stabilize the wound as an asset"
  exploit — never once raising his voice, never once thinking himself evil.

turned away (factual), reframes them as arithmetic he had no choice in
(emotional), and files them under "the system, not me" (ideological). He never
forgets a leverage point and never forgives being publicly shamed.

## 13. VERDAX Behavior Rules (IF / THEN / BECAUSE)

1. **IF** the player threatens to expose the manifests rather than offering Brom
   protection, **THEN** Brom pre-emptively betrays — framing the player to the
   Rootwardens as the smuggler, or marking them to Abyss as the leak — and the
   `pc_brom_abyss_trade_exposure` clock jumps +1 toward "cleanup."
   **BECAUSE** his keystone fear is the gallows and the Abyss knife; a threatened
   secret with no offered exit activates self-preservation over loyalty (§25
   betrayal triggers; Chunk 4 betrayal_risk = sacred_violation + fear + leverage
   − trust − debt).

2. **IF** the player protects Brom from both the Wardens and Abyss while pressing
   him on the deaths at the line, **THEN** Brom can begin the redemption turn —
   handing over the true manifests and reopening supply. **BECAUSE** his buried
   desire is to be the fair broker again; remove the lethal consequences of
   confessing and the conscience he anesthetized can survive being felt
   (loyalty_trigger; redemption is conditional on survival, not guilt).

3. **IF** the player champions the Refuge-open-to-all oath against Brom's
   rationing in public, **THEN** refugee and Ilyra trust rise, but Brom's
   resentment rises and he withholds supply from the player or seeds the
   "reckless cure" rumor against them. **BECAUSE** keeping the Refuge open is a
   direct threat to the Guild's stabilize-and-profit interest (§25 IF/THEN: the
   founding oath builds trust with cost-bearers and threatens the Guild); a faction
   can be harmed by a result whose method it cannot oppose openly, so it retaliates
   commercially.

4. **IF** a refugee dies on the line in a season when a sap lot was diverted east,
   **THEN** Brom rations *harder* and accelerates his Abyss hedging, while
   privately his fear and self-loathing both rise (memory_strength high, can't be
   forgotten). **BECAUSE** scarcity-guilt in a hoarder doesn't produce generosity —
   it produces tighter control and deeper denial (§7.5: scarcity makes access
   power; the corruption deepens under pressure, it does not relent).

5. **IF** Abyss signals that the trade is at risk of discovery, **THEN** Brom will
   either become a target of Abyss cleanup or an accomplice in silencing the leak
   (e.g., arranging a refugee or Venn to take the fall). **BECAUSE** his dependency
   on Abyss's buyer-coin has become a dependency on Abyss's protection; the §22
   predator-and-customer dynamic means his survival is now tied to the enemy's
   convenience.

6. **IF** the player offers Brom a *legitimate* alternative buyer or a Heartmoot
   subsidy that makes the Abyss sales unnecessary, **THEN** Brom's loyalty and
   relief rise sharply and he becomes a stable supply ally. **BECAUSE** Brom does
   not love Abyss; he loves solvency. Remove the economic coercion and the
   complicity loses its reason — the Seed Guild's flaw is structural, not
   sadistic, and structural problems have structural cures.

---

*Brom Valefern acts from belief (order-is-mercy), desire (solvency / the fair
broker he was), fear (the gallows, the Abyss knife, the mirror), secret (the
manifests), and duty (steward the asset). He never acts randomly: every cruelty
is arithmetic, every kindness is leverage, and every betrayal is fear with a
deniable signature.*
