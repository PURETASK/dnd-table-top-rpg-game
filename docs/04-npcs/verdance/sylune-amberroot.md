# Sylune Amberroot — *The Healer Who Cannot Bear to Wait*

> "They tell me to be patient. Be patient. I have buried eleven people this season
> who would be alive if 'patient' were a medicine. The well *can* be healed. I know
> how. The only thing standing between these people and a cure is a roomful of
> elders deciding, again, that the time to decide is not yet. Tell me to be patient
> one more time and watch what I do."
> — Sylune Amberroot, to the player, in the sap-house

## Name
Sylune Amberroot. Called *Amberroot*, *the Healer of Thornveil*, *Healer-of-Renown*.
The refugees call her *the one who doesn't turn you away*.

## Domain
Verdance — Thornveil Refuge. Leader of the Sap Commons and the region's foremost
healer.

## Public Role
Sap Commons leader and **healer-of-renown** — the brilliant restorationist who runs
the sap-house, treats the refugees the Seed Guild would ration away, and champions
the position that the corrupted network can be **cured, not just contained**. She is
the moral engine of "heal it, don't just hold it," and the most respected restorer
in the region.

## Private Role
The campaign's **best hope for a real cure** — and its most dangerous source of a
*premature* one. She holds the actual restoration knowledge that could heal the well
(with the Heartroot cuttings and time), and she is exactly one funeral too many away
from forcing a half-ready cure that could spread the blight. The player's natural
ally who needs the player to keep her *patient enough to succeed*.

## Faction Ties
- **Sap Commons** — her movement, her people, her platform; she leads it by
  competence and love, not title.
- Against the **Heartmoot** (who decides, and whether to heal at all — Maera's
  paralysis is the wall Sylune beats herself against) and the **Seed Guild** (commons
  vs. commodity — Brom's rationing starves her patients).
- In respectful tension with the **Rootwardens** (Thalen) over cure-vs-contain — they
  agree on the cardinal taboo, disagree on whether the wall should ever come down.
- Allied in spirit with **Ilyra** (whose dead Sylune wants released) and wary-hopeful
  of **Auralis** (whose hearing the cure may need, whose slide Sylune fears).

## Belief Core
*Suffering you can end and choose not to is suffering you caused. The network is a
living thing; living things can be healed; refusing to try is not patience, it is
abandonment with better manners.* Sylune believes restoration is an obligation, not
an option — and that the Verdant worship of patience has become a permission slip for
letting people die. She is *right* about the abandonment. She is *dangerous* about the
timing.

## Desire
To **cure the well** — to release the trapped, restore the soil, and break the
scarcity that's killing her patients — and to do it *now, with her own hands*, before
one more grave is dug. Beneath the urgency: to never again stand helpless at a
deathbed that didn't have to be one.

## Fear
That she will run out of time — that while she waits for consensus and cuttings, the
corruption spreads past the point any cure can reach, and she'll have spent her one
chance being *patient*. Her fear is the mirror of Maera's: Maera fears acting too
soon, Sylune fears acting too late, and the Keeper feeds on both.

## Secret
She has already **attempted a small, unsanctioned restoration** on a peripheral
corrupted root — and it *half-worked*, then partly failed, leaving a patch of soil
worse than before. She has hidden this, because admitting it would prove the elders'
"a reckless cure spreads the blight" point and cost her the cure's credibility — but it
also means she knows, in a part of herself she won't face, that her cure is **not
ready**, and she's terrified that knowing isn't enough to stop her. (Visibility:
hidden → hinted if the player finds the worsened patch; partial if romanced.)

## Public Mask
Warm, fierce, tireless, plainspoken; the healer who remembers every patient's name
and never makes them feel like a burden. Her conviction is magnetic — people *believe*
in the cure because they believe in her. The mask is almost entirely true; the crack
is the desperation underneath the conviction.

## Private Truth
She is grieving, constantly, and running on it. She keeps the names of her dead the
way Thalen keeps his lost wardens. Her certainty about the cure is partly real
expertise and partly a wall against the unbearable thought that some of these people
*can't* be saved. She is closer to forcing a failing cure than even she knows, because
to her, doing *something* wrong feels more bearable than doing *nothing* right.

## Relationship Function
The player's **natural ally for a real cure** — the partner the restoration path runs
through, source of healing expertise, popular legitimacy, and the genuine (if
dangerous) road to healing the well. But she is also a person the player must
**steady**: keep her patient enough that the cure *works* and stop her from forcing a
half-ready one that fails. As a romance, she is the warmest of the candidates and the
one whose love most directly collides with the player's methods (corrupted sap will
fracture the bond; forcing a cure may fracture the player's trust in *her*).

## Can Romance? Betray? Die? Kill? Defect?
- **Romance:** **Yes** — the warm, devoted, working-side-by-side Verdant kind; a
  graftbond partner if the bond survives the crisis. Loving Sylune means sometimes
  being the hand on her arm that says *not yet*.
- **Betray:** **No** in the treacherous sense — but she can **defy the player and the
  Rootmoot** by forcing the cure against counsel, which *feels* like a betrayal of the
  patient path even though it comes from love. She does not deceive for gain.
- **Die:** **Yes** — a potential martyr (dies *attempting* the cure, spending herself
  to heal, the Verdant tender's death) or a tragic radical's death (killed by the
  failing cure she forced).
- **Kill:** Only by **forced healing** — overriding consent, rushing a Passage, or
  imposing a cure that kills (the corruption of her own virtue, 7.3). She wields no
  weapon; her harm comes wearing the face of help.
- **Defect:** **No** to the Hollow Pact (she *despises* weaponized rot — it is
  everything she fights). Her "defection" is to **radicalism within restoration**:
  forcing the cure, becoming the tragic zealot whose compassion outruns her caution.

## Rumor Style
Sylune's word is trusted (healer-of-renown is a trusted source) and she uses it
**bluntly and morally** — she names the abandonment out loud ("the Refuge is starving
its refugees"; "the elders sealed wells and hid it"), which spreads fast and
righteous. She doesn't spin so much as *indict*, and her indictments can outrun her
proof. A grieving, furious Sylune can seed a rumor that radicalizes the Commons.

## Lie Style
She is honest to a fault in public and lies mainly **by hiding her own failure** (the
botched peripheral cure) and by **overstating her readiness** to herself and others.
Her lie is the optimist's lie: "it's ready, it'll work, trust me" — said to the dying,
and half-said to silence her own doubt.

## Loyalty Trigger
She bonds — fiercely, lastingly, perhaps in love — with a player who **fights for the
cure WITH her** while helping her do it *right*: who pursues the cuttings, breaks
Maera's paralysis, defends the Refuge oath, refuses the corrupted shortcut, and — the
hard part — stands with her in the patience that makes the cure actually work. A
player who treats her urgency as righteous *and* her caution as necessary earns her
whole heart.

## Betrayal Trigger
Two things turn Sylune against the player. First, **using corrupted sap / soul-sap** —
the cardinal sin, the thing she fights; she will oppose the player publicly even if she
loves them (love does not erase the cardinal sin). Second, **siding with the elders'
paralysis or the Guild's rationing** — letting her patients die for "stability" reads
to Sylune as the deepest betrayal of all, and can radicalize her *away* from the player
and toward forcing the cure alone.

## Death Consequence
**(Depth 4.)** The restoration path loses its leader and its credibility; the Sap
Commons fractures or radicalizes; the cure becomes far harder.
- **Martyr's death (spends herself completing/attempting the true cure):** could inspire
  a careful continuation — her people finish what she started, patiently, in her name;
  the cure becomes sacred legacy.
- **Tragic radical's death (killed by a forced, failing cure):** the Commons panics
  into the Hollow Pact's fast alternative; her death becomes a cautionary horror;
  "restoration kills" enters the rumor ecology and the containment faction is grimly
  vindicated.
- **Murdered (Guild, Pact, or amid the chaos of exposure):** the Commons goes to war;
  her name becomes a banner; the player's relationship to her death (avenger, mourner,
  betrayer) reshapes their reputation.

## Hero Arc
*The True Cure.* Steadied by the player and given the cuttings and the time, Sylune
leads the **cleansing-communion** that heals the well *the right way* — braided with
Auralis's hearing and the Rootwardens' containment — releasing the trapped, restoring
the soil, and breaking the scarcity that was killing her patients. She survives,
changed and vindicated, the healer who proved patience and restoration are partners,
not enemies. Her botched secret cure becomes the lesson that made the real one work.

## Villain Arc
*The Forced Cure.* One funeral too many, denied the cuttings, betrayed by the
paralysis, Sylune **forces a half-ready restoration on the well** to save the dying
*now* — and it spreads the blight. She becomes the tragic radical her own virtue
warned of (Forced Healing, 7.3): a healer who could not bear to wait, whose love
became recklessness, whose cure became a wound. She does not become cruel — she becomes
*desperate*, and desperation in a healer of her power is its own catastrophe. If she
survives it, she is broken by what she did; if she doesn't, the failing cure kills her.

## Agenda Clock
**"One Funeral Too Many" (clock_type: opportunity-turned-corruption, current 2 / 6.)**
- **Tick +1:** each patient who dies for lack of clean sap; each time Maera defers; each
  time the Seed Guild's rationing turns someone away; each season the cuttings stay
  locked; each time the player sides with caution *without* giving her a path to act.
- **At 6 (completed):** Sylune forces the half-ready cure (the villain/tragic-radical
  arc) — it spreads the corruption, vindicates containment, and may kill her; the Sap
  Commons radicalizes or shatters.
- **Reversal methods:** breaking Maera's paralysis and releasing the cuttings (gives
  her readiness a real timeline); proving the slow cure viable in small (a true
  Passage-completion, e.g., Rook's parent); defending the Refuge oath so her patients
  stop dying of scarcity; the player steadying her with *partnership*, not just
  restraint; addressing the Abyss source so the well stops worsening faster than she
  can heal it. **Critically:** telling her "be patient" without giving her a path ticks
  the clock UP, not down — paralysis is what radicalizes her.

## Memory Fields
Sylune remembers through **the dead she couldn't save** — every event filed against the
ledger of preventable loss. Her memory is accurate and humane, but **urgency-weighted**:
she remembers most vividly the moments where waiting cost a life, and least vividly the
moments where haste would have. She forgets nothing about a patient and forgives nothing
about the abandonment.
- *factual_memory:* who she treated, who she lost, what the cure attempt did.
- *emotional_interpretation:* grief, fury at the paralysis, fierce tenderness for her
  patients, a buried terror that she isn't ready.
- *ideological_interpretation:* "they call it patience; it is abandonment"; and the
  dangerous corollary, "doing something wrong is more bearable than doing nothing
  right."
- can_be_forgotten: false (a healer remembers her dead).
- can_be_distorted: moderate — toward urgency (the cost of waiting looms larger in
  memory than the cost of haste).
- can_be_erased: false.

## VERDAX Behavior Rules

> Sylune never acts randomly. She acts from her **belief that preventable suffering is
> caused suffering**, her **desire to cure now with her own hands**, her **fear of
> running out of time**, and her **secret** that her cure isn't ready — and the Forced
> Healing corruption (7.3) is her specific danger.

1. **IF** the player fights for the cure *with* Sylune — pursuing the cuttings, breaking
   Maera's paralysis, defending the Refuge oath — *and* helps her do it right, **THEN**
   she bonds fiercely (trust +10, affection +8, loyalty +10, "One Funeral Too Many"
   clock reversal) and the restoration path opens fully, **BECAUSE** her deepest need is
   a partner who treats her urgency as righteous and her caution as necessary — that
   partnership is the only thing that makes patience bearable to her.

2. **IF** the player tells Sylune to "be patient" or sides with the elders' paralysis /
   the Guild's rationing *without* giving her a path to act, **THEN** the "One Funeral
   Too Many" clock ticks +1 and she drifts toward forcing the cure alone (trust −5,
   resentment +8, radicalization risk +), **BECAUSE** to Sylune, counseled patience with
   no path is just abandonment with better manners — it confirms that no one will act, so
   she must, even half-ready.

3. **IF** the player forces or encourages a half-ready cure on the well (or pushes her
   to act before she's ready), **THEN** it may fail and spread the corruption; even if it
   "works," Sap Commons trust drops and the Rootwardens feel vindicated about containment
   (soil corruption +, "One Funeral Too Many" clock +2, depth-4 chain), **BECAUSE**
   forced/hasty healing is a Verdant corruption — haste in sacred matters breaks the
   cure and the trust; the cycle cannot be rushed.

4. **IF** the player uses corrupted sap / soul-sap, **THEN** Sylune opposes them publicly
   even if she loves them, and (if romanced) the bond fractures politically while the
   love may persist privately (trust −18, affection −6, suspicion +15), **BECAUSE**
   weaponized rot is the cardinal sin and everything she fights — love does not erase
   ideology, and a healer cannot abide the deliberate spreading of the wound she's trying
   to close.

5. **IF** the player discovers Sylune's secret botched cure and brings it to her *as a
   lesson to make the real cure safe* rather than as ammunition to discredit her, **THEN**
   she is shaken but relieved and accepts a slower, safer protocol (trust +8, the buried
   "I'm not ready" doubt surfaces healthily, clock reversal), **BECAUSE** her secret's
   worst weight is the fear that she'll repeat the failure at scale — being met with help
   instead of judgment lets her admit the readiness gap and close it.

6. **IF** the player completes even one true, patient release or restoration in small
   (e.g., Rook's parent, a cleansed peripheral root), **THEN** Sylune's whole arc bends
   toward the hero path (hope +, "One Funeral Too Many" clock reversal, she commits to
   the patient protocol), **BECAUSE** living proof that the slow true way *works* gives
   her urgency a clean channel and refutes the despairing logic that drives her toward
   forcing it — she can wait if she can believe the waiting will pay.
