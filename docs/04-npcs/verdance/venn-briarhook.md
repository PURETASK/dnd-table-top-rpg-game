# Venn Briarhook — *The Map That Sells Itself*

> "Everybody at this border's got a wall they're hiding behind. Wardens got the
> thornwall, elders got their 'next season,' Brom's got his ledger. Me? I'm the
> one who knows where all the gaps are. Information's the only sap that never runs
> dry — and I'll sell you a cupful at a fair price. The whole barrel costs more."
> — Venn Briarhook, over cards in a Thornveil waystation

---

## 1. Identity

- **Name:** Venn Briarhook
- **Domain:** Verdance (border-bred; loyal to no map and every road)
- **Public Role:** Seed Guild caravan-master — runs sap, seed, and grown-goods
  trains along the Green Roads and the Eastern Thornwall waystations; nominally a
  Guild logistics contractor.
- **Private Role:** Information-broker and smuggler-courier. Venn is the person who
  actually *knows* the border's secrets: who crosses where, who buys soul-sap,
  where the Abyss supply line runs, the location of the Hollow Reach, and the truth
  of the three sealed-and-denied wells. The roots remember everyone's footsteps;
  Venn remembers what the roots can't say out loud, and sells it.

Lean, quick, never seated with their back to a door, perpetually mid-deal. Wears a
courier's mycelial relay-band and a gambler's smile. Loyal mainly to themselves
(§21) — which is not the same as having no loyalties at all, a distinction the
player can exploit or fatally misread.

## 2. Faction Ties

- **Primary (loose):** Seed Guild — Venn carries Guild manifests and takes Guild
  coin, but the Guild is a client, not a creed.
- **Real network:** Everyone. Venn sells to the Rootwardens (intelligence), the
  Sap Commons (smuggled clean sap), Brom (cover for the diverted lots), the Hollow
  Pact (routes, on a bad day), and — at one remove, through deniable middlemen —
  to Abyss fronts.
- **The line (so far):** Venn moves sap, seed, people, and *information*. Venn has
  not, yet, moved **soul-sap** or **trapped names as commodity**. That refusal is
  the last load-bearing wall of their self-image. The Void wants that wall.

## 3. Inner Engine (belief / desire / fear / secret / duty)

- **Belief Core:** *Survival is the only honest religion; everyone is selling
  something, so be the best-informed seller in the room.* Venn believes loyalty is
  a luxury the border can't afford and has been priced accordingly. They are not a
  nihilist — they are an exhausted realist who has watched every idealist at this
  frontier get harvested, sealed, or buried.
- **Desire:** To stay ahead — of debts, of armies, of the corruption creeping up
  the roads — and to retire alive and warm, somewhere the wells don't talk. Beneath
  the mercenary patter is a tired person who wants, very badly, to stop running.
- **Fear:** Being *owned.* Venn fears the Hollow Pact and Abyss not morally but
  practically — both buy people whole, and a courier who's been bought whole stops
  being a courier and starts being a tool. Deeper: Venn is afraid the well will
  one day say a name they recognize, and that they'll be standing close enough to
  hear it.
- **Secret:** Venn knows the **physical route of the Abyss soul-harvest supply
  line** and the **approximate location of the Hollow Reach** — and has known for
  longer than is safe to admit. Venn has sat on it because selling it to the wrong
  buyer gets Venn killed, and selling it to the right one means picking a side, and
  picking a side is the one thing Venn swore never to do.
- **Duty (self-imposed):** To the deal. Venn keeps a bargain to the letter — it is
  the only honor they have left, and it is real. A paid-for promise is kept; an
  unpaid one was never made.

## 4. Masks

- **Public Mask:** The cheerful mercenary; the gambler with a fair price; the
  rogue who'd sell their grandmother but would tell you the going rate first. All
  surface, all transaction, nothing to hold.
- **Private Truth:** Venn is far more frightened, far more tired, and far closer to
  caring than the patter admits. The mercenary mask is armor against a border that
  has taught them that everyone they don't sell first will eventually sell them.
  Treated as a *person* rather than a vendor, the armor has seams.

## 5. Relationship Function

Venn is the **intelligence engine** of the MVP (§21 Intelligence Figure): the
player's source of smuggling routes, contacts, and the three load-bearing leads —
the **Abyss connection**, the **Hollow Reach**, and the **sealed wells**. Venn is
also a *reading test*: unreliable, double-selling, reachable by the Void on a bad
day — but capable of surprising loyalty if treated as a person rather than a map
to be folded up and pocketed. Venn teaches the player that information has a price
and that the cheapest way to buy it is sometimes respect.

## 6. Capability Flags

- **Can Romance?** Low / situational. Not a designed romance candidate, but a
  player who earns the real person behind the mask may find a wary, guarded warmth.
  Default: no.
- **Can Betray?** **Yes — a primary betrayer/double-seller (§21).** Venn sells the
  player's movements to whoever pays better, unless given a reason not to.
- **Can Die?** Yes. Death takes the border's intelligence and smuggling routes dark
  (§21 death_impact).
- **Can Kill?** Yes, reluctantly — Venn fights to escape, not to win, and would
  rather pay someone or talk their way out. A cornered Venn is dangerous precisely
  because they've stopped negotiating.
- **Can Defect?** **Yes — to whoever pays/protects best, OR to the Void on a bad
  day** (§9: pragmatic survivors most likely to accept). Venn's defection is the
  crossing of the last wall: moving soul-sap, selling trapped names.
- **Can Start Rumors?** Yes — and is one of the *best* at it (controls the mycelial
  relay; §24).

## 7. Voice & Distortion

- **Rumor Style:** *Calibrated leakage through the relay.* Venn doesn't gossip;
  Venn *places* information. A true thing, leaked to the right ear at the right
  price, moves armies and markets. Venn's signature is using the mycelial relay
  (§24) to seed a rumor that's mostly true — which is more dangerous than a lie,
  because it can't be refuted, only repriced. Venn can also corrupt the relay into
  *false* rumor when paid to.
- **Lie Style:** *The true-but-incomplete sale.* Venn rarely lies outright — it's
  bad for business. Instead Venn sells you a real fact and lets you assume the rest,
  or sells you the *first half* of a secret and quotes a price for the second. The
  player who pays for "the Abyss line runs through the eastern waystations" and
  assumes they've bought the whole route has been honestly cheated.

## 8. Triggers

- **Loyalty Trigger (what earns Venn):** Being treated as a person, not a vendor —
  paying fairly *and* once choosing not to use leverage when Venn was vulnerable;
  keeping a bargain to Venn when it cost the player something. Venn's honor is the
  deal; prove you honor *your* end past convenience and Venn quietly upgrades you
  from client to the rarest thing they have — someone they'd warn before selling.
- **Betrayal Trigger (what breaks Venn):** A better offer with no countervailing
  relationship; being cornered into picking a side they didn't choose; a threat to
  *own* them. If the player tries to coerce or blackmail Venn instead of paying,
  Venn sells the player out on principle — coercion is the thing they exist to
  refuse (§25: faction pressure exceeding loyalty; the Void's shortcut at the
  breaking point).

## 9. Consequence Standards

- **Death Consequence (§21 death_impact; Chunk 4 death doctrine):** If Venn dies,
  the border's **intelligence and smuggling routes go dark.** The player loses their
  fastest path to the Abyss line, the Hollow Reach, and the sealed wells — unless
  Venn already sold those leads, in which case the secrets either *surface*
  (someone Venn told talks) or *vanish* (the trail dies with the only person who
  had the whole map). The border information economy reshuffles violently: whoever
  Venn was selling to loses a source or scrambles to cover their tracks, and a
  power vacuum opens that the Hollow Pact or Abyss may fill. *The dead matter here
  because Venn was a hub: kill the hub and the whole network's wiring shorts.*
- **Hero Arc (Chosen Side):** *The Courier Who Picked.* Treated as a person and
  given a cause worth the risk, Venn finally crosses the line they always refused —
  not into the Void, but into commitment. They guide the player to the Abyss
  incision and the Hollow Reach, run the clean sap to the refugees Brom denied, and
  use the relay to spread the *truth* of the sealed wells. It costs them their
  carefully-priced neutrality and probably their safe retirement. It is the bravest
  deal they ever refused to charge for.
- **Villain Arc (Bought Whole):** *The Void's Best Courier.* On a bad enough day —
  cornered, in debt, with a buyer who pays in both coin and the promise of finally
  being *safe* — Venn crosses the last wall. They move soul-sap. They sell trapped
  names as commodity. The Void's lie to Venn isn't grand; it's exhausted and
  practical: *"Stop running. Let it pay you. The dead don't care who carries them."*
  A Venn who's been bought whole becomes the supply artery of the Hollow Reach and
  the most dangerous informant against the player.

## 11. Memory Fields

Venn's memories are filed like ledgers but felt like wounds: every deal logged by
price and party (factual), every coercion remembered as a threat to their freedom
(emotional), every kindness-without-leverage flagged as suspicious and then,
slowly, as precious (ideological). Venn forgets nothing that has a price and
forgives only those who proved they wouldn't use a debt against them.

## 13. VERDAX Behavior Rules (IF / THEN / BECAUSE)

1. **IF** the player tries to coerce or blackmail Venn for a secret instead of
   paying, **THEN** Venn sells the player out — leaking their plans through the
   relay to a buyer who'll pay — and `pc_venn_picks_a_side` ticks toward the
   anti-player completion. **BECAUSE** Venn's keystone fear is being *owned*, and
   coercion is exactly the ownership they exist to refuse; the deal is their only
   honor, and a forced "deal" is no deal at all (§25 betrayal trigger: faction/
   personal pressure exceeding loyalty).

2. **IF** the player pays fairly AND, at a moment of leverage, chooses not to use
   it, **THEN** Venn's trust, respect, and debt rise, and they begin warning the
   player before selling them — the first sign of real loyalty. **BECAUSE** Venn
   keeps a strict mental ledger; a kindness-without-leverage is an anomaly that, once
   logged, can promote a client into the one name in the "warm column" (loyalty_
   trigger; trust built by patience and proven good faith, §25 trust_is_built_by).

3. **IF** a soul-sap or trapped-name buyer offers Venn both coin and the promise of
   *safety* while Venn is in debt or cornered, **THEN** Venn hesitates at the last
   wall (void_resonance flickers; clock ticks toward Void completion) and will cross
   it unless a built relationship or cleared debt gives them a reason to refuse.
   **BECAUSE** Venn is the §9 archetype — the pragmatic survivor — and the Void's
   Verdance lie to them is not grandeur but exhaustion: *stop running, let it pay
   you.* Safety is the bribe that reaches a tired person no horror can.

4. **IF** the player has earned Venn's loyalty and then asks for the Abyss line /
   Hollow Reach / sealed-wells leads, **THEN** Venn gives them — guiding rather than
   merely selling — and crosses into commitment (hero arc). **BECAUSE** Venn's honor
   IS the kept bargain; once the player is in the warm column, the bargain extends
   past price into a side freely chosen, which is the only kind Venn ever respected
   in others.

5. **IF** the player pays Venn for a secret but does not pay for the *whole* secret,
   **THEN** Venn delivers exactly what was bought and lets the player assume the
   rest — an honest cheat that may send the player into the Hollow Reach with half a
   map. **BECAUSE** the true-but-incomplete sale is Venn's lie style; they protect
   their value by metering it, and an unpaid assumption was never their lie to
   correct (lie_style; information is the sap that never runs dry only if rationed).

6. **IF** Venn dies before selling their three big leads, **THEN** those leads go
   dark and the player must rebuild the intelligence path the slow way (through the
   roots, the Wardens, or the Communion). **BECAUSE** Venn is a network hub, not a
   document; the knowledge lived in a person, and §21's death_impact makes the
   border's wiring short when the hub is killed (Chunk 4: death changes the world
   when the dead were load-bearing).

---

*Venn Briarhook acts from belief (survival-realism), desire (to stop running),
fear (being owned; the well's recognized name), secret (the Abyss line and the
Hollow Reach), and duty (the kept bargain). Venn never acts randomly: every leak
is priced, every betrayal is a refused ownership, and the one unpriced thing —
warning a friend before selling them — is the loyalty the player has to earn.*
