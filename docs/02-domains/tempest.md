> **Canon discipline.** This bible obeys Chunk 1 (Master Canon Foundation), follows the 31-section structure of Chunk 2 (Domain Bible Template), uses the state language of Chunk 3 (VERDAX Tracking Glossary), and reaches the consequence depth required by Chunk 4 (Cause/Effect Standards). Tempest is **not** "the sky/storm land." Tempest is a metaphysical civilization whose economy, religion, military doctrine, class order, and legitimacy all turn on one sacred idea: **freedom is sacred and loyalty must be chosen, never forced.** Its greatest virtue is also its open wound — the same belief that makes it free makes it exploitable.

**One-paragraph essence.** Tempest is a confederacy of sky-clans riding skyships and bonded dragons through and around the Maelstrom Barrier — a perpetual ring-storm that both walls the domain off and feeds its magic. Its people believe a bond is only real if freely chosen, and that to force loyalty (on a person, a dragon, or a clan) is the deepest spiritual crime, *even when forcing it would save a life.* This belief produces breathtaking freedom and breathtaking blind spots: corsairs who price evacuation by the seat, riders who abandon a duty mid-war because "no one has the right to command me," and a fringe — the Chainbreaker Radicals — who have taken the sacred idea to its logical poison and reject *all* duty as slavery. The Keeper does not need to make Tempest evil. It only needs to make Tempest believe that every restraint, every promise, every shared burden is a chain.

## 2. Hard Canon

These are locked. No faction, NPC, or campaign may contradict them.

1. **Freedom is sacred; chosen loyalty is the only true loyalty.** A bond, oath, or service that is compelled is metaphysically *false* and spiritually defiling — to the binder and the bound.
2. **The dragon-rider bond is a partnership of two free souls,** not ownership and not a mount relationship. A dragon chooses its rider; the rider does not capture a dragon. Either may, in principle, walk away — and a bond that cannot be walked away from is already broken.
3. **Forced loyalty is a spiritual violation even when it saves lives.** This is the Dragon-Bond Sanctum's hardest teaching and Tempest's deepest tension. Binding a dragon to keep it from flying into a lethal Void-storm *saves the dragon and violates bond-law simultaneously.* Both are true.
4. **The Maelstrom Barrier is real, perpetual, and partly conscious as weather, not as a mind.** It rings the domain, gates entry, feeds storm magic, and grows more violent as soul flow is disrupted.
5. **Storm magic draws from three braided sources** — Storm Cores (Stone-fragments), bonded dragons, and the Maelstrom Barrier (see §4 for the locked resolution).
6. **The unbonded majority resent the bonded elite,** and this resentment is structural, not petty — it is a permanent fault line the Keeper works.
7. **Tempest does not hold ground.** Its civilization, military, and economy are built on motion; a Tempest that stops moving is a Tempest already losing.
8. **Skrehaven and the Skymoot are real:** the Skymoot is an elected, rotating clan-assembly, not a throne. Tempest has no king and is offended by the idea.
9. **The Chainbreaker Radicals exist** as a fringe that takes "freedom is sacred" to mean "no duty, no bond, no restraint binds anyone" — they are Tempest's self-poison, not outside invaders.
10. **Kaida is a bonded rider who values bond-law over affection;** her dragon **Azyr** refuses to fly under commanders who tolerate forced loyalty. Both are canon NPCs.

## 3. Metaphysical Role

Tempest carries the **freedom / chosen-bond** principle released when the pure Nexus Stone was fractured by the Architect Sacrifice (Chunk 1 §2.4, §2.6). In the lattice of principles that hold the Keeper's prison shut, Tempest is the strand of **uncoerced will** — the living proof that a soul can choose loyalty without compulsion. This is precisely what the Keeper hates: the Keeper believes "free will is inefficiency" and "identity is a lock waiting to be opened" (Chunk 1 §2.3). Every freely chosen bond in Tempest is a small refutation of the Keeper's worldview, and therefore a small reinforcement of the prison.

This makes Tempest's role **double-edged in the prison-lattice:**

- **When chosen bond is healthy,** Tempest stabilizes the part of reality that says *consent is real and loyalty can be given freely.* Clean dragon-rider bonds are among the strongest anti-Keeper structures in the world, because they are voluntary soul-partnerships that no one owns.
- **When chosen bond corrupts into "no one may restrain me,"** Tempest becomes a generator of oath-rejection and anti-responsibility — and oath corrosion is one of the Keeper's named strategies (Chunk 1 §2.5). A domain that treats *every* promise as a chain dissolves the reality-law that oaths mean something. The Keeper does not even have to break Tempest's oaths; it only has to convince Tempest that oaths are slavery.

**Why Tempest matters to VERDAX:** Tempest is the domain where the player most directly confronts the question *"Does freedom include the right to refuse responsibility?"* Every storm Tempest rides, every dragon it bonds, every evacuation it prices, is also a referendum on whether sacred freedom has become sacred selfishness.

### The locked decision on the source

> **LOCKED:** Storm magic draws from **all three** — Storm Cores, bonded dragon-souls, and the Maelstrom Barrier — and they are **a single fractured source in three forms,** not three separate batteries. They are **coupled:** coercion and oath-rejection in any one form destabilize the others. This is why "forced loyalty makes the storm worse" is literally true in Tempest, not merely a moral metaphor.

This resolution is deliberate: it makes Tempest's central moral claim (forced bond is defiling) **mechanically real** in the world-state, so VERDAX can model "the player bound a dragon → Barrier surge +N → sky-route closes → evacuation prices spike" as a genuine causal chain rather than flavor.

**Key insight for VERDAX:** in Tempest, *the cardinal soul-wound is not too little freedom but freedom turned against the very bonds and oaths that give a soul shape.* A forced bond and a totally unbound soul are **both** wounds — one is a locked door, the other is a soul with no doors at all. The healthy state is a soul with **doors it chooses to open.**

## 8. Keeper Exploit Pattern

```ts
KeeperExploitPattern {
  domain: "Tempest"
  primary_virtue_targeted: "Freedom (and its child, Chosen Bond)"
  secondary_virtues_targeted: ["Risk", "Dragon Partnership", "Motion"]
  corruption_strategy: "Convince every faction — separately, in its own language — that responsibility is a chain. Tell the corsairs that regulation is tyranny, tell the riders that command is slavery, tell the grieving that bonds are wounds, tell the elite that the unbonded are lesser, and tell the Chainbreakers that they alone have understood freedom. Each faction then defends 'freedom' in a way that fractures the domain, rejects its oaths, and sours its Stone — and each blames the others, never the shared creed."

  preferred_crisis_conditions: [
    "war pressure that makes 'just bind the dragon' tempting (forced loyalty entry point)",
    "evacuation crises where freedom-to-charge clashes with duty-to-rescue",
    "betrayals that radicalize the betrayed into oath-rejection",
    "elite-commoner resentment spikes after a Choosing or a denied rescue"
  ]
  preferred_faction_targets: [
    "Chainbreaker Radicals (already most of the way there)",
    "Cloudreach Corsair Concord (greed dressed as freedom)",
    "Stormwarden Air-Corps (war-pressure toward bond domination)"
  ]
  preferred_npc_targets: [
    "the betrayed (toward oath-rejection)",
    "the grieving (toward possessive love / bond domination)",
    "the desperate commander (toward forcing a dragon)",
    "the proud high-house youth (toward clan arrogance)"
  ]
  soul_flow_damage_type: ["broken_oath", "soul_binding", "death_passage_blocked", "identity_dissolution"]

  domain_stone_damage_type: "Coupled feedback: forced bonds + mass oath-rejection → Barrier surges, Storm Cores sour, bonded dragons sicken. The Keeper damages all three braided sources at once by working both extremes."
  common_lie: "No one has the right to stop me, because freedom is sacred."
  long_term_goal: "A Tempest where freedom means 'no one owes anyone anything,' so its oaths dissolve (lattice damage), its bonds break or are forced (soul-binding wounds), its dragons revolt or are enslaved, and its perpetual Barrier becomes a permanent killing storm — a domain that has talked itself into self-destruction while feeling heroic the entire time."
}
```

**The trap, stated plainly.** Tempest's genuine truth — *that forced loyalty is defiling* — is correct. The Keeper does not deny it. The Keeper **extends** it: from "you may not force a bond" to "no one may bind you to anything," from "loyalty must be chosen" to "no chosen loyalty can be held against you later." The slide from *consent* to *anti-responsibility* is the entire exploit. A free soul keeps its promises *because it chose them.* A Keeper-poisoned soul breaks its promises *because it can.* They look identical for exactly one moment — the moment of choice — and opposite ever after.

## 9. Void Temptation Pattern

```ts
VoidTemptationPattern {
  domain: "Tempest"
  temptation_phrase: "If freedom is sacred, no one has the right to restrain you. (And its twin: 'Love that cannot restrain lets death win — so bind it, just this once.')"
  immediate_benefit: "Real power and real rescue: the dragon survives the Void-storm because you bound it; the mission succeeds because you overrode the refusal; the grief stops because you cut the tie."
  hidden_cost: "Every forced bond is a soul-binding wound on two souls; every cut tie thins the binder's own identity; the Tri-Braid registers it and the Barrier surges. The shortcut works once and asks again, louder."

  identity_damage: "The binder's selfhood erodes (you become someone who forces; that is now who you are). The cut-loose soul (Chainbreaker path) dissolves toward shapelessness."
  soul_flow_damage: "soul_binding (forced bond) and/or identity_dissolution (cut ties) and/or death_passage_blocked (denied Wind-Release)."
  reality_damage: "Barrier surge → sky-routes close → stranded clans → evacuation-price spikes → resentment → more oath-rejection → lattice fray → Keeper pressure."

  faction_most_likely_to_accept: ["Corsair Concord (the 'restraint just this once' tools)", "war-pressed Air-Corps commanders", "Chainbreaker Radicals (the 'cut every tie' twin)"]
  npc_archetypes_most_likely_to_accept: ["the desperate rider", "the grieving partner", "the betrayed turned cynic", "the commander out of options"]

  visible_stage_1_symptoms: ["a single 'just this once' forced restraint", "a single mocked oath", "a dragon that flinches once"]
  visible_stage_2_symptoms: ["restraint-tools normalized", "Wind-Release rites skipped", "the dragon's eyes going flat", "the binder defending the practice"]
  visible_stage_3_symptoms: ["Sanctum bond-trials convened", "Azyr-type dragons refusing the commander", "Barrier surges traced to the practice", "the binder unable to imagine *not* forcing"]
}
```

> **The two-faced Void.** Tempest is unusual: the Void tempts it from **both poles.** To the desperate it says *bind* (forced loyalty); to the grieving and the betrayed it says *cut every tie* (anti-responsibility). Both are "freedom." Both are wounds. A VERDAX campaign should make the player feel that *both* temptations are reasonable in the moment — because they are. The point is not that restraint is always wrong (binding the dragon really does save it). The point is that the cost is never clean, and Tempest's creed makes it dangerously easy to pretend the cost isn't there.

**The evacuation-pricing fault line (campaign-critical).** When an Abyss raid or a Barrier surge dooms a skyport, the only lift out is corsair skyships. The Concord prices the seats. The bonded fly themselves out for free. The unbonded who can't pay are left. *This is legal in Tempest* — no authority can compel a free captain to fly anyone. It is also the single most reliable radicalizer in the domain: every abandoned commoner becomes a Chainbreaker recruit or an Abyss defector, and every defense of the practice ("a captain's deck is his own; freedom is sacred") is the Keeper's lie wearing a respectable coat. A VERDAX campaign should treat any evacuation crisis as a depth-4-minimum consequence engine.

## 29. Cause/Effect Examples (≥5, reaching 3rd–5th order)

> Each chain uses **Action → Method → Witness → Interpretation → Power Shift → Social Reaction → Systemic Effect → Metaphysical Effect → Future Pressure** (Chunk 4), with explicit intervention paths.

### 29.A — Bind the dragon to save it from a Void-storm (the canon chain)

**Root:** An injured bonded dragon, panicked and storm-sick, is flying straight toward a lethal Void-storm cell inside the Barrier. The player **binds/restrains it** (a forced override of its will) to stop it. *(Depth 5 — touches soul flow, the Stone-source, bond-law.)*

- **Method/texture:** protective AND coercive at once. The dragon lives; the bond is violated.
- **1st order:** Dragon survives; the rider's gratitude +15; the **dragon's trust −20**; bond-law stress +8; a hairline soul_binding scar forms on both souls.
- **2nd order:** The Dragon-Bond Sanctum hears of the restraint and opens a bond-trial; the Chainbreaker Radicals weaponize it ("see — even the 'good' ones cage dragons; all bonds are chains"); **Kaida** questions whether the player understands freedom at all.
- **3rd order:** Bonded riders split into "would-do-the-same" and "never" camps; under-tier commoners cry hypocrisy ("the elite cage dragons but lecture us"); **corsairs start selling restraint-tools**, citing the player's example as precedent.
- **4th order:** A planned sky-alliance mission is delayed by the Sanctum-Corps rift the event widened; **Azyr refuses to fly under any commander who endorsed the binding**; Abyss anti-air fortifies the gap while Tempest argues with itself.
- **5th order (metaphysical):** The **Keeper benefits** as freedom fractures into *control* (bond-domination) vs *anti-duty extremism* (Chainbreaker), each blaming the other; the Tri-Braid registers the forced bond and the **Barrier surges**; the **Void whispers** *"Love that cannot restrain lets death win"* — and to the Chainbreakers, *"You see? Cut every tie."* Keeper pressure +; soul-flow integrity −.
- **Interventions:** submit to a Sanctum bond-trial; give the dragon a public voice (let it speak through Kaida/Azyr); find a consent-restoring healing (Maelis); expose Chainbreaker exaggeration; help the rider release the control and re-choose the bond cleanly. *(Per Chunk 4 §C — heavy but playable.)*

### 29.B — Compel a free evacuation during a Barrier surge

**Root:** A Barrier surge dooms an under-tier skyport. Corsairs are pricing the seats; the poor will be left. The player **convinces (or pressures) the Skymoot to compel free evacuation.** *(Depth 4 — faction politics, the creed, the economy.)*

- **Method:** protective but coercive of free captains — a creed-violation in service of survival.
- **1st order:** Hundreds of commoners lifted free; **commoner gratitude soars** (Old Bram, Tessa, the under-tiers); Concord profits lost; the **no-conscription creed visibly bent**.
- **2nd order:** Hesper Vane and the Concord declare tyranny; free-contract clans grow wary; **Chainbreakers crow** "see — your Skymoot is just another chain, exactly as we said."
- **3rd order:** The Skymoot's legitimacy splits — saviors to the poor, oath-breakers to the corsairs; recruitment to BOTH the populist bloc AND the Chainbreakers rises (for opposite reasons); a precedent now exists for compulsion.
- **4th order:** The Concord retaliates by hoarding sky-routes and weather-windows; trade frays; the next crisis finds the Skymoot trapped — compel again (more legitimacy loss) or not (mass death).
- **5th order:** The **Keeper wins on both horns** — compulsion vindicates the Chainbreaker "all duty is a chain" creed (oath-rejection rises) while abandonment would have mass-produced defectors; soul-flow strained either way; the Void offers the Skymoot a "Storm-Marshal with real power" (a step toward the very tyranny Tempest fears).
- **Interventions:** broker a **voluntary Rescue-Honor accord** (captains earn standing/toll-relief for lifting the abandoned — making rescue a *chosen* duty, freedom-compatible); shame-pressure via rumor; expose Concord profiteering; fund the lift so no compulsion is needed.

### 29.C — Expose the Tollwidow's Abyss network

**Root:** The player uncovers and **publicly exposes the Tollwidow** — a Concord broker secretly buying soul-bound Tempest dragons for Abyss and selling restraint-tools that enable bond-domination. *(Depth 5 — reframes the domain's corruption, touches soul-binding & Abyss.)*

- **Method:** truth-telling, disruptive and dangerous.
- **1st order:** The restraint-tool supply collapses; the Concord is rocked; the player gains massive Sanctum & rider respect; the Tollwidow and her Abyss handlers mark the player a **blood-enemy**.
- **2nd order:** The revelation reframes Tempest's forced-loyalty epidemic as **partly an Abyss op**, not just home-grown rot — the Sanctum-Corps Interdict War cools (it was external sabotage all along); but exposed corsairs scramble to bury their involvement.
- **3rd order:** Some Chainbreakers are horrified to learn Abyss has been **harvesting the cut-loose** (Tovic's arc); a faction of radicals turns on the Abyss infiltrators; others double down in denial. The under-tier rumor "the bonded force dragons" loses fuel.
- **4th order:** Tempest unity rises against a clear external enemy; the Air-Corps and Sanctum coordinate raids on Abyss soul-binding ops; the Concord must publicly disavow the trade or fracture.
- **5th order:** **Keeper pressure drops** as division heals into shared resolve and freed dragons re-affirm chosen bond (a rare anti-Keeper victory) — BUT Abyss escalates anti-air and the Void whispers to the now-marked player that they "had to break some rules to get the truth out." Watch for a tactical-success-with-a-new-front.
- **Interventions / follow-through:** free the soul-bound dragons cleanly (offer, don't demand, re-bonding); protect witnesses; bring Tovic and wavering radicals home; ensure the Concord reform is real, not cosmetic.

### 29.D — Win an aerial battle with Void-amplified stormcalling

**Root:** Losing a desperate gate-defense, the player **uses Void-amplified stormcalling** to summon an overwhelming storm with no fatigue and rout the Abyss anti-air. *(Depth 5 — Void, Stone-source, soul flow.)*

- **Method:** liberating in the moment, corruptive underneath — power without partnership.
- **1st order:** The gate holds; the wing is saved; Tempest reputation +; the player's **void_resonance +4**; a Storm Core sours; a small Barrier ripple.
- **2nd order:** Barrier-Warden Ilya traces the surge to the player's storm; the Air-Corps celebrates the save; the Sanctum grows alarmed; a rumor spreads that "the new flier commands storms without asking the sky."
- **3rd order:** War-pressed commanders ask **how to replicate it**; the Concord's hedge-schools sniff a market; clean stormcallers feel suddenly weak by comparison — the doctrine tilts toward Void-shortcuts.
- **4th order:** If replicated, Stormcalling slides from partnership toward command; Cores sour faster; the Barrier surges more often, closing routes and **spiking evacuation prices** (feeding chain 29.B); Verdance and Sanctuary grow wary of Tempest's "forced storms."
- **5th order:** The **Keeper benefits** — a heroic victory created Void dependency, a soured Core, a surging Barrier, and the seed of a doctrine that strips consent from Tempest's magic (the exact mechanism that makes it clean). The Void's future whisper: *"It worked. The sky obeyed. Why ever ask again?"*
- **Interventions:** publicly name the danger; submit the act for Sanctum review; refuse to replicate; help Ilya and the wardens define safe limits; credit the wing's skill, not the storm; resist the next whisper.

### 29.E — Honor a dragon's refusal at tactical cost (the clean choice)

**Root:** Mid-raid, a dragon (Azyr, or another) **refuses an order** it judges dishonorable — bombing a skyport with civilians. The player **honors the refusal**, aborting the strike at real tactical cost. *(Depth 3–4 — bond-law, faction, doctrine.)*

- **Method:** honorable, transparent, costly — the textbook clean act.
- **1st order:** The strike is aborted; the civilians live; the mission's military objective is missed; the commander (if a bond-dominator) is furious; **Azyr's and the Sanctum's respect for the player rises sharply**.
- **2nd order:** Clean-bond riders rally to the player as someone who truly gets chosen bond; bond-domination commanders mark the player as unreliable; a rumor spreads — "a flier who lets dragons say no."
- **3rd order:** The Interdict War tilts toward the Sanctum's side (here's living proof honoring refusal works); some war-pressed riders, shamed, reconsider their own overrides; the Air-Corps debates whether "a dragon's no" is insubordination or wisdom.
- **4th order:** Tempest's clean-bond strength rises; dragons across wings grow more willing to fly under commanders who respect refusal (a real military force-multiplier — willing partners outfly forced ones); the doctrine shifts toward partnership.
- **5th order:** **Keeper pressure drops** — a freely-honored "no" reinforces the reality-law that consent is real, strengthening the prison-lattice; the Tri-Braid steadies slightly; the Void finds no foothold here. This is the rare chain that *heals.* (VERDAX should reward it as such — clean, but the missed objective is a genuine cost, not a freebie.)
- **Interventions / amplification:** publicize the success to strengthen bond-law; protect the player from the dominator-commander's retaliation; use the moment to broker the Interdict War's peaceful resolution (§23.1).

## 30. Open Questions

1. **Dragon population & origin:** How many dragons exist? Are they born free and later choose, or hatched near clans? Can a dragon Choose more than once in a lifetime? What happens to a dragon whose rider dies — does it re-Choose, go wild, or grieve to death?
2. **The Barrier's nature:** Is the Maelstrom Barrier truly *only* weather coupled to soul-flow, or is there a fragment of conscious Architect-principle within it? Could it ever be "spoken to" rather than merely read?
3. **Core souring & supply:** Are Storm Cores genuinely finite (a slow extinction) or do they regenerate in the Barrier? If finite, Tempest faces a long-term magical decline that reframes everything — is this a campaign clock?
4. **The Choosing's fairness:** Is the Choosing truly random/consent-based, or do bloodline and privilege secretly tilt it (which would make clan-arrogance partly *true*)? The answer determines whether the elite-commoner divide is injustice or just luck.
5. **Chainbreaker endgame:** Can a fully "cut-loose" soul be healed, or is identity-dissolution a one-way door past a certain point? Is Marrek redeemable or already gone?
6. **Renewable oaths vs the lattice:** Does Tempest's *renewable* (non-permanent) oath culture actually weaken the reality-law of binding-promises (mild lattice strain), or is a freely-renewed oath as strong as an eternal one? This affects whether Tempest's creed is metaphysically costly even when healthy.
7. **The "even when it saves lives" absolute:** Is the Sanctum's hardest teaching *literally* metaphysically correct (a forced bond is always a wound), or is there a clean emergency-restraint rite that restores consent without leaving a scar? (Resolving this resolves the Interdict War.)
8. **Cross-domain bonds:** Can a non-Tempest character (a Radiance or Sanctuary player) ever be Chosen by a dragon, and what would that mean politically and metaphysically?

## 31. Locked Decisions

1. **Core belief is locked:** "Freedom is sacred, and loyalty must be chosen — never forced." It is Tempest's strength and its exploitable wound.
2. **True virtues (locked):** freedom, risk, storm mastery, chosen bond, dragon partnership, motion, courage under uncertainty.
3. **Corrupted forms (locked):** entitlement, recklessness, anti-responsibility, possessive love, bond domination, clan arrogance, oath rejection.
4. **Keeper exploit (locked):** convince every faction that responsibility is a chain — "No one has the right to stop me, because freedom is sacred."
5. **Void lie (locked):** "If freedom is sacred, no one has the right to restrain you" — with its twin, "bind it to save it."
6. **The Tri-Braid (locked):** storm magic draws from **all three** — Storm Cores, bonded dragon-souls, and the Maelstrom Barrier — as **one fractured source in three coupled forms.** Forced loyalty and oath-rejection literally destabilize the Stone-source and surge the Barrier.
7. **Forced loyalty is a spiritual violation even when it saves lives (locked).** This is the Sanctum's hardest teaching and the engine of Tempest's central dilemma; binding the dragon to save it is *both* a rescue and a violation.
8. **Government (locked):** an elected, rotating **Skymoot** with no permanent throne, a separate spiritual **Dragon-Bond Sanctum** holding bond-law and the interdict, deliberately too weak to compel — by design.
9. **Class structure (locked):** bonded dragon-elite vs unbonded commoners is a permanent, structural fault line, with the Sanctum-Sworn and the cast-loose as the other two classes.
10. **Military doctrine (locked):** "The Moving Storm" — mobile, aerial, raid-and-vanish; **Tempest never holds ground.**
11. **Economy (locked):** food-import-dependent; built on sky-routes, skyship freight, cloud-markets, corsair trade, storm-core mining, and the scandalous **evacuation pricing.**
12. **The five core factions (locked):** the Skymoot, the Dragon-Bond Sanctum, the Stormwarden Air-Corps, the Cloudreach Corsair Concord, and the Chainbreaker Radicals — each with a real point and a dangerous flaw.
13. **Chainbreaker Radicals (locked):** Tempest's self-poison — the creed taken to "all bonds and oaths are chains," ending in identity-dissolution and (ironically) Abyss soul-chains.
14. **Kaida & Azyr (locked):** Kaida values bond-law over affection and will turn on a beloved player who forces a dragon; Azyr refuses to fly under commanders who tolerate forced loyalty. Both are canon mechanics, not flavor.
15. **Abyss is the existential enemy (locked):** Abyss soul-binding is the literal inverse of chosen bond, and Tempest's own corruptions (bond-domination, evacuation-greed, oath-rejection) are Abyss's doorway in — embodied by the Tollwidow.
16. **Death custom (locked):** the **Wind-Release** — the dead are freed into the storm, never held; denying it is a cardinal soul-crime.
17. **Consequence standard (locked):** forced bonds, Void-amplified stormcalling, denied Wind-Release, and soul-binding are **depth-5** events; evacuation and faction politics are **depth-4** minimum.
