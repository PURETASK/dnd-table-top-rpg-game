> **One-line truth:** Radiance is not "fire people." Radiance is a sacred-fire
> *military civilization* whose economy, religion, legitimacy, succession, and war
> doctrine all depend on the Eternal Flame, the Phoenix rebirth rites, Sunstone
> forgecraft, and a culture that believes **true power must be paid for through
> willing sacrifice.**

**Snapshot in prose.** Radiance is the domain that pays. Where Sanctuary protects
and Abyss takes, Radiance *offers* — and has built an entire civilization around
the conviction that nothing sacred is free. Its soldiers march under banners of a
rising phoenix; its forges never go cold; its priests are also veterans; its
nobility is a caste of those who have burned something of themselves and survived.
The Eternal Flame in the Pyraeum is simultaneously its god, its power grid, its
treasury anchor, and its constitution. The domain is strong, proud, and brave —
and that very pride is the lever the Keeper and the Void reach for.

## 2. Hard Canon

These elements are **locked** and must not be contradicted. They may be expanded.

1. **Radiance is a sacred-fire military civilization.** Its core belief: *true
   power must be paid for through willing sacrifice.*
2. **True virtues:** sacrifice, renewal, courage, sacred fire, honor, rebirth,
   discipline.
3. **Corrupted forms:** hubris, martyr addiction, purge doctrine, forced rebirth,
   hero spectacle, military ego, shame culture.
4. **Keeper exploit:** confuse sacrifice with glory — "If I am willing to die,
   whatever I do must be holy."
5. **Void lie:** "If your sacrifice is pure, why fear burning more?"
6. **The Eternal Flame** is the domain's living sacred fire, anchored in a
   **Sunstone Heart** — a flame-form fragment of the fractured pure Nexus Stone.
7. **The Phoenix** and **Phoenix rebirth rites** are real, sacred, and rare — a
   controlled exception in soul flow, not casual resurrection.
8. **Kael** is a celebrated hero/champion figure. His **military bloc pushes
   high-output relic / "champion-device" research** and is a source of internal
   fracture.
9. **The Sunstone** drives industry, weapons, and forgecraft.
10. **The Ember Council** is the ruling and spiritual authority.
11. **The Dawnfire Mystics** are the spiritual/magical authority, anxious about
    purity versus spectacle.
12. **The Forgekeepers** are the Sunstone forge guild — an economic and military
    power.
13. **The Ashen Martyrs** are a dangerous fringe martyr-cult.
14. **The Sunbreaker War Doctrine** is the creed of the military hardliners.
15. **The domain Stone source is tied to the Eternal Flame / a Sunstone Heart.**
16. Per Chunk 1: each domain's greatest virtue is also its primary corruption
    vulnerability; the Keeper corrupts by **overcommitment**, not rejection; the
    Void is **enhancement, parasite, and liar**; Abyss is coherently evil with the
    Keeper as its root.

## 3. Metaphysical Role

In the metaphysics of Chunk 1, the original Architects each carried a fundamental
principle. Radiance carries the **principle of willing sacrifice and renewal** —
the law that energy, change, and rebirth in reality must be *paid for* by
something freely given, and that what is paid is not destroyed but transformed.

When the Architects sacrificed themselves and fractured the pure Nexus Stone to
imprison the Keeper, the principle of sacrifice could not be stored as cold stone
— it could only be held as **living fire**. So Radiance's fragment did not
crystallize; it ignited. The Eternal Flame is what a Nexus-Stone fragment looks
like when its principle is *the act of giving itself away.* This is why Radiance's
magic, more than any domain's, mirrors the original Architect Sacrifice in
miniature: every flame-rite is a small re-enactment of the founding act that keeps
the Keeper out.

**Why Radiance matters to the prison.** The prison's stability depends partly on
*uncoerced sacrifice* and *clean rebirth* (Chunk 1 §2.5, §2.7). Radiance is the
domain that practices both at civilizational scale. Healthy Radiance is therefore
a load-bearing pillar of the prison: every honest sacrifice and every clean
Phoenix rebirth reinforces the founding logic that locks the Keeper away. This is
also why Radiance is such a high-value target. If the Keeper can make Radiance
confuse *sacrifice* with *glory*, *renewal* with *purge*, and *rebirth* with
*forced rebirth*, then the very domain that re-enacts the prison's founding act
will instead re-enact its breaking — sacrifice without consent, fire without cost,
rebirth without death. Corrupted Radiance does not merely weaken the prison; it
*mimics the founding act while inverting it*, which the Keeper prizes above almost
any other exploit.

**Soul-flow significance.** Radiance is one of the only domains with a sanctioned
soul-transformation pathway (Phoenix rebirth). This makes it a guardian of one of
soul flow's most delicate operations and, simultaneously, the domain whose
mistakes can produce the most dangerous soul-wounds: **false rebirth** and
**void_amplification** of sacred fire.

to all but Council rite. Kael's champion-devices are dangerous precisely because
they tap closer to the Heart than doctrine allows — trading long-term Stone
stability for short-term battlefield output.

**The Phoenix exception.** Phoenix rebirth is canon and real, but it is a *narrow,
expensive exception* to soul flow, not a respawn button. A true Phoenix rebirth
requires: (1) genuine, complete death; (2) the dying soul's prior consent; (3) a
sanctioned rite witnessed by Dawnfire Mystics; (4) a worthy cause that the Flame
"accepts." When all four hold, the soul passes and returns transformed with memory
and identity intact. When any is faked or forced, the result is **false rebirth** —
the single most dangerous soul-wound Radiance can produce, and the exact thing the
Void and Abyss most want Radiance to start doing routinely.

## 6. True Virtues

Each virtue below is a `DomainVirtue`: healthy definition plus cultural, heroic,
political, magical, economic, and military expressions, and a common saying.

### 6.1 Sacrifice
- **healthy_definition:** Freely giving something you value — time, comfort, safety,
  body, or life — so that something worthier may live or change. The cost must be
  *real to the giver* and *willingly chosen*.
- **cultural_expression:** Citizens "lay an ember" — a personal cost offered at a
  hearth — to mark grief, gratitude, or resolve. Children are taught the difference
  between giving and showing-off-giving.
- **heroic_expression:** A hero who pays the price *themselves* rather than spending
  others. The mark of a Radiant hero is a cost no one demanded.
- **political_expression:** Rank and franchise are tied to witnessed sacrifice; the
  Ember Council's seats are held by those who have given measurably and honestly.
- **magical_expression:** Every flame-rite is fueled by an offering. The strongest
  Emberweaving is the most *honestly costly*, not the loudest.
- **economic_expression:** The "ember-tithe" — labor, goods, or service given to the
  Flame and forges; tithe-honesty is audited by the Mystics.
- **military_expression:** Officers are expected to absorb risk before their
  soldiers do; commanders who spend lives without sharing the cost lose honor.
- **common_saying:** *"Fire that costs you nothing warms no one."*

### 6.2 Renewal
- **healthy_definition:** The faith that what is given is transformed, not lost —
  ash becomes soil, death becomes warmth, defeat becomes resolve.
- **cultural_expression:** The Dawnlighting each morning; rebuilding burned things
  better than before is a civic pride.
- **heroic_expression:** Rebuilding after loss; refusing to let a sacrifice be wasted.
- **political_expression:** Post-defeat reform is legitimate; "the renewal mandate"
  lets a disgraced leader earn back standing through rebuilding.
- **magical_expression:** Phoenix renewal magic; re-tempering broken sacred steel.
- **economic_expression:** Salvage-and-renew forge cycles; nothing burned is wasted.
- **military_expression:** Doctrine of the second wave — a broken line reforms hotter.
- **common_saying:** *"From the ash, a hotter dawn."*

### 6.3 Courage
- **healthy_definition:** Acting rightly while afraid; facing the fire instead of
  spending others to face it for you.
- **cultural_expression:** Public "ember-trials" of nerve at coming-of-age.
- **heroic_expression:** Walking into the burning thing first.
- **political_expression:** Speaking unwelcome truth to the Council is honored as
  "cold courage."
- **magical_expression:** Drawing flame in defense of others despite the personal scar.
- **economic_expression:** Forgekeepers who work the dangerous inner seams.
- **military_expression:** The Sunbreaker charge — the front rank are volunteers.
- **common_saying:** *"Be the first to burn."*

### 6.4 Sacred Fire
- **healthy_definition:** Reverence for flame as a living principle to be *served*
  and disciplined, never merely *used* — fire that remembers it is a gift.
- **cultural_expression:** No hearth is ever spat in; flame is greeted, not commanded.
- **heroic_expression:** Using fire with restraint, sparing what need not burn.
- **political_expression:** Only the consecrated may light the great rites.
- **magical_expression:** Disciplined Emberweaving that burns exactly what it must.
- **economic_expression:** Consecrated forging; sacred steel commands premium trade.
- **military_expression:** Targeted sacred ordnance, not indiscriminate burning.
- **common_saying:** *"The Flame is a guest, not a tool."*

### 6.5 Honor
- **healthy_definition:** Living so your word, rank, and fire can be trusted; owning
  your costs publicly; refusing glory you did not pay for.
- **cultural_expression:** Honor-scars worn openly; lying about a sacrifice is the
  deepest shame.
- **heroic_expression:** Declining undeserved praise; crediting the dead.
- **political_expression:** Oath-by-ember binds officials; broken ember-oaths end careers.
- **magical_expression:** Never claiming another's offering as your own fuel.
- **economic_expression:** Honest tithe and honest forging; counterfeit "Phoenix-blessed" goods are a capital fraud.
- **military_expression:** Quarter offered to the honorably beaten; no burning of the surrendered.
- **common_saying:** *"Carry your own scar."*

### 6.6 Rebirth
- **healthy_definition:** The sacred, consented transformation of death into renewed
  life or purpose — death fully met, then transformed, with the self intact.
- **cultural_expression:** The Ashening returns the dead to the Flame; Phoenix rites
  are domain-wide holy days, rare and awe-struck.
- **heroic_expression:** Consenting to die for a cause *and* trusting renewal, not
  demanding it.
- **political_expression:** Phoenix-Reborn carry unique moral authority (and danger).
- **magical_expression:** Phoenix rites; re-consecration of the fallen.
- **economic_expression:** Phoenix-blessed relics and ashes are sacred trade goods.
- **military_expression:** A Phoenix-Reborn champion can rally a broken army.
- **common_saying:** *"Die true, and the Flame returns you."*

### 6.7 Discipline
- **healthy_definition:** The trained restraint that keeps fire, courage, and
  sacrifice *holy* instead of wild — the spine that turns passion into devotion.
- **cultural_expression:** Daily forms; the cold morning drill; measured speech.
- **heroic_expression:** Holding the line; not burning when burning would feel good.
- **political_expression:** Rule of rite; even the Council bows to the rite-calendar.
- **magical_expression:** The "banked flame" — power held and shaped, not spilled.
- **economic_expression:** Forge-craft precision; tolerances measured to the hair.
- **military_expression:** The Sunbreaker formations; fire on command, never before.
- **common_saying:** *"Bank the flame; let it choose its hour."*

## 7. Corrupted Virtues

Each is a `CorruptedVirtue`. The corruption formula (Chunk 1 §5): **True Virtue →
Crisis Pressure → Extreme Interpretation → Faction Split → Social Harm → Soul-Flow
Damage → Keeper Advantage.**

### 7.1 Hubris (from Sacred Fire / Honor)
- **corrupted_definition:** The belief that one's fire is so pure it cannot err —
  that being Radiant *is* being right.
- **how_it_begins:** A great victory that "proves" a leader's flame is favored.
- **how_it_spreads:** Through praise; the favored stop submitting rites for review.
- **who_benefits:** Demagogue commanders; Kael's bloc; the Void (which loves
  unchecked casters).
- **who_suffers:** Soldiers spent on certainties; civilians burned "for their good."
- **faction_most_vulnerable:** Sunbreaker hardliners; the champion-device bloc.
- **npc_archetypes_most_vulnerable:** decorated champions, young prodigies, victorious priests.
- **keeper_benefit:** A caster who cannot be corrected can be steered.
- **void_lie:** "Your fire has never failed — why ask permission?"
- **visible_symptoms:** ["skipping rite-review", "claiming personal favor of the Flame",
  "punishing dissent as faithlessness"]
- **late_stage_consequence:** A champion declares his own judgment equal to the
  Council's and ignites a schism.

### 7.2 Martyr Addiction (from Sacrifice)
- **corrupted_definition:** Craving the *feeling* and *status* of sacrifice; chasing
  death and pain as proof of worth, regardless of need.
- **how_it_begins:** A grieving or shamed person finds that offering pain earns love.
- **how_it_spreads:** Through cults like the Ashen Martyrs that reward escalating burns.
- **who_benefits:** Ashen Martyrs; recruiters; the Void (martyr-pressure = instability).
- **who_suffers:** The addicted; their families; those pressured to "match" them.
- **faction_most_vulnerable:** Ashen Martyrs; the grieving lower castes.
- **npc_archetypes_most_vulnerable:** the bereaved, the disgraced, the unloved young.
- **keeper_benefit:** Each addicted sacrifice is a soul-scar and a spectacle-burn.
- **void_lie:** "If your sacrifice is pure, why fear burning more?"
- **visible_symptoms:** ["seeking suicide-missions", "self-burning as devotion",
  "shaming the cautious as cowards"]
- **late_stage_consequence:** Ember-haunts — souls that burned for spectacle and
  will not pass, demanding fresh deaths.

### 7.3 Purge Doctrine (from Renewal)
- **corrupted_definition:** Renewal twisted into cleansing — the belief that to make
  something new you must first *burn away the impure*, including people.
- **how_it_begins:** A reform framed as "burning out the rot."
- **how_it_spreads:** Crisis sermons; "we lost because we were impure."
- **who_benefits:** Purity hardliners; informers; Abyss (which loves Radiance making refugees).
- **who_suffers:** Minorities, dissenters, the merciful, neighboring domains.
- **faction_most_vulnerable:** Dawnfire Mystic *extremists*; Sunbreaker command.
- **npc_archetypes_most_vulnerable:** zealous inquisitor-priests, frightened officials.
- **keeper_benefit:** Renewal-as-destruction is the founding act inverted.
- **void_lie:** "If decay must be burned, why spare the decayed?"
- **visible_symptoms:** ["purity lists", "burning of 'impure' districts", "renaming
  destruction as renewal"]
- **late_stage_consequence:** Internal purges that fracture the domain and create
  refugee streams Abyss harvests.

### 7.4 Forced Rebirth (from Rebirth)
- **corrupted_definition:** Rebirth without true death or consent — dragging souls
  back, or pushing them through the rite for power, producing false rebirth.
- **how_it_begins:** A beloved champion dies; grief or strategy demands his return *now*.
- **how_it_spreads:** Each "success" (a body that lives) hides the hollow self within.
- **who_benefits:** Commanders needing heroes; Abyss (false rebirth = harvestable wounds).
- **who_suffers:** The falsely reborn (a self chained to fire it never chose); their loved ones.
- **faction_most_vulnerable:** Kael's bloc; desperate Sunbreaker command.
- **npc_archetypes_most_vulnerable:** grief-stricken commanders, ambitious rite-masters.
- **keeper_benefit:** Mass false rebirth corrupts soul flow's most sacred exception.
- **void_lie:** "He was willing once — that consent still counts."
- **visible_symptoms:** ["rites performed in haste/secret", "reborn who don't recognize
  loved ones", "Mystics excluded from the rite"]
- **late_stage_consequence:** A hollow Phoenix-Reborn champion — beloved, powerful,
  and not truly *anyone* — used as a banner.

### 7.5 Hero Spectacle (from Courage / Sacred Fire)
- **corrupted_definition:** Courage performed for audience and propaganda; fire as
  show. Spectacle replaces devotion (Chunk 1 §3).
- **how_it_begins:** A victory staged for morale that "works."
- **how_it_spreads:** Through war-theater, champion-pageants, recorded burns.
- **who_benefits:** Propagandists; Kael's image-handlers; the Void.
- **who_suffers:** Quiet servants overshadowed; truth; soldiers sent to make good footage.
- **faction_most_vulnerable:** Kael's champion-device bloc; the war-press.
- **npc_archetypes_most_vulnerable:** rising champions, image-managers, young glory-seekers.
- **keeper_benefit:** Spectacle teaches that the *look* of sacrifice can replace the *cost*.
- **void_lie:** "If it inspires them, the cost doesn't matter."
- **visible_symptoms:** ["staged sacrifices", "champion cults", "burning for cameras not need"]
- **late_stage_consequence:** A generation that confuses being watched-burning with being holy.

### 7.6 Military Ego (from Honor / Discipline)
- **corrupted_definition:** Rank-pride that treats the army's glory as the domain's
  purpose; officers who see soldiers as fuel.
- **how_it_begins:** Wartime elevation of the Sunbreaker command above rite and Council.
- **how_it_spreads:** Through emergency powers that never get returned.
- **who_benefits:** Sunbreaker hardliners; war-profiteer Forgekeepers.
- **who_suffers:** Conscripts; civilians; the Council's legitimacy.
- **faction_most_vulnerable:** Sunbreaker War Doctrine command.
- **npc_archetypes_most_vulnerable:** generals, quartermasters, war-heroes.
- **keeper_benefit:** Militarized Radiance spends its own people as ammunition.
- **void_lie:** "Victory is the only honor that survives."

## 11. Magic Costs

Each is a `MagicCost`. Radiance's defining trait: it is *proud* of its costs. The
danger is when the cost is hidden, deferred, coerced, or spent from someone else.

### 11.1 The Offering (cost_type: life_force)
- **description:** The core fuel of all honest flame-craft — a measure of the
  caster's own vitality and something they value, freely given.
- **minor_effect:** Fatigue, a day's weakness, a small honor-scar.
- **moderate_effect:** Lasting exhaustion; a permanent scar; loss of a treasured thing burned in offering.
- **severe_effect:** Life-threatening depletion; the caster's hair greys, the body
  ages; in the greatest rites, death (sometimes the intended cost).
- **recovery_method:** Rest, the Dawnlighting's renewing warmth, honest community care.
- **permanent_risk:** Over-giving thins the soul; chronic over-offering shades into martyr addiction.
- **factions_that_accept_this_cost:** ["Dawnfire Mystics", "Ember Council", "honest Emberweavers"]
- **factions_that_reject_this_cost:** ["Kael's bloc (prefers device-output over personal cost)"]

### 11.2 Heart-Strain (cost_type: domain_stone_strain)
- **description:** Drawing power closer to the Sunstone Heart than honest rite —
  the cost paid by the *Stone*, not the caster. Champion-devices run on this.
- **minor_effect:** Brief flame fatigue in nearby hearths.
- **moderate_effect:** Ash-bleed on local consecrated steel; a faint Heart-scar.
- **severe_effect:** A cooled slag-seam in the Heart; domain_stone_stability drop; whisper-embers.
- **recovery_method:** Mystic re-consecration; a season of honest sacrifice; rarely, Verdance re-cooling.
- **permanent_risk:** Cumulative Heart-scarring lowers architect_sacrifice_integrity — prison erosion.
- **factions_that_accept_this_cost:** ["Kael's bloc", "Sunbreaker command in crisis"]
- **factions_that_reject_this_cost:** ["Dawnfire Mystics", "Forgekeeper traditionalists"]

### 11.3 Soul-Scar (cost_type: soul_integrity)
- **description:** The price of touching soul-fire — Phoenix rites, deep oaths,
  death-passage work. Paid honestly, it is a sacred mark; paid through coercion or
  haste, it is a wound.
- **minor_effect:** A "warm shadow" — mild identity blur for days.
- **moderate_effect:** Soul-scar; recurring dreams in another's voice; reduced soul_integrity.
- **severe_effect:** Identity dissolution (martyr-self loss) or false rebirth in others.
- **recovery_method:** Name-restoration rites; proper Ashening; Mystic soul-healing.
- **permanent_risk:** Hollow self; ember-haunting after death.
- **factions_that_accept_this_cost:** ["Dawnfire Conclave (under sanction)", "Ashen Martyrs (recklessly)"]
- **factions_that_reject_this_cost:** ["Ember Council (outside sanctioned rite)"]

### 11.4 Void Dependency (cost_type: void_dependency)
- **description:** The compounding hidden cost of overcharge — the Void's interest rate.
- **minor_effect:** A craving to "burn a little more"; whisper-embers nearby.
- **moderate_effect:** Inability to feel powerful without overcharge; rising void_resonance.
- **severe_effect:** Cannot cast without the Void; insists old limits were superstition.
- **recovery_method:** Purification at the Pyraeum; abstinence under Mystic witness; very hard.
- **permanent_risk:** Permanent void_resonance; becoming a whisper-ember's mouthpiece.
- **factions_that_accept_this_cost:** ["nobody openly — it is always disguised as devotion"]
- **factions_that_reject_this_cost:** ["all official factions reject it in name"]

### 11.5 The Ember-Tithe (cost_type: resource_consumption)
- **description:** The economic cost — Sunstone ore, fuel, labor, and consecrated
  materials spent in forging and rite.
- **minor_effect:** Routine guild expense.
- **moderate_effect:** Sunstone scarcity in wartime; rationing of consecration.
- **severe_effect:** Forge-famine; the Dawnlighting itself rationed; civilian unrest.
- **recovery_method:** New seams, salvage-renewal cycles, trade for raw star-metal.
- **permanent_risk:** Strip-mining the Heart's outer seams toward the forbidden inner Heart.
- **factions_that_accept_this_cost:** ["Forgekeepers", "Ember Council"]
- **factions_that_reject_this_cost:** ["civilian castes when rationing bites"]

### 11.6 Shame-Debt (cost_type: social_trust)
- **description:** The social cost of *visible* sacrifice culture — those who cannot
  or will not burn lose standing; honor becomes a currency that can bankrupt.
- **minor_effect:** Mild loss of face for the un-scarred.
- **moderate_effect:** Exclusion; pressure to "prove" oneself.
- **severe_effect:** Martyr-pressing; the shamed driven to the Ashen Martyrs.
- **recovery_method:** Reform of honor-ranking; public valuing of quiet sacrifice.
- **permanent_risk:** A full shame culture that breeds martyrs.
- **factions_that_accept_this_cost:** ["Ashen Martyrs (weaponize it)", "status-keepers"]
- **factions_that_reject_this_cost:** ["Dawnfire reformers", "merciful Council members"]

## 20. Faction Index

```ts
DomainFactionIndex { domain: "Radiance", factions: [ ... ] }
```

Six factions across the required archetypes. Each has a **real point** and a
**dangerous flaw.**

### 20.1 The Ember Council — *central authority*
- **short_doctrine:** "Rule by honest, witnessed sacrifice; the Flame's acceptance is
  legitimacy; discipline keeps fire holy." Custodians of rite, law, and the great Flame.
- **class_base:** Embersworn; senior Mystics; old forge-lords.
- **power_base:** legitimacy (the Flame "accepts" them), law, the rite-treasury, the
  Bankfire Guard, broad public faith.
- **main_rival:** Kael's champion-device bloc (rising prestige) and, in wartime, Sunbreaker Command.
- **keeper_vulnerability:** institutional dishonesty — hiding the Heart-scarring to
  prevent panic teaches the domain that *truth about sacrifice can be managed*, the
  first crack the Keeper widens.
- **player_use:** the lawful patron; grants writs, legitimacy, access to rite and Pyraeum;
  can sanction or condemn the player's flame-use.
- **Real point:** without the discipline of rite and law, Radiance's sacrifice-culture
  would burn out of control overnight. The Council is the spine that keeps fire holy.
- **Dangerous flaw:** to protect stability it conceals the Heart-scarring and past
  overcharge data — a managed lie that corrodes the very honesty the domain rests on.

### 20.2 The Dawnfire Mystics — *spiritual/magical authority*
- **short_doctrine:** "Guard the purity of the Flame and the cleanness of soul-passage;
  sacrifice must be honest, rebirth must be true, fire must be disciplined."
- **class_base:** the priesthood; rite-masters; Ash-Keepers; Purifiers.
- **power_base:** religion, magic (Phoenix rites, purification), moral authority over
  what is 'pure,' the Conclave's confirmation power over Council seats.
- **main_rival:** Kael's bloc and the overcharge/spectacle tendency across the war faction.
- **keeper_vulnerability:** purity-zeal can curdle into the *purge heresy* — extremist
  Mystics who decide renewal requires burning the impure (people included).
- **player_use:** spiritual guides, purifiers, rebirth-judges; the player's path to
  healing soul-wounds, judging contested rites, and resisting the Void.
- **Real point:** they are the conscience of the domain — the only faction structurally
  devoted to keeping sacrifice *honest* and rebirth *true*, and the front line against
  Void overcharge and false rebirth.
- **Dangerous flaw:** their guardianship of purity can tip into purity-policing, shame,
  and (at the extreme) purge doctrine — protecting the Flame by burning the people.

### 20.3 The Sunbreaker War Doctrine Command — *military/security*
- **short_doctrine:** "Overwhelming sacred force; pay the price first, win the field,
  renew from the ash. In war, doctrine leads." The military hardliners.
- **class_base:** the officer corps; Sunbreaker legions; war-veterans of the Scarred.
- **power_base:** military strength, frontier control (Cindergate), wartime emergency
  powers (the Kindling Decree), the loyalty of soldiers.
- **main_rival:** the Ember Council (over emergency powers) and the Dawnfire Mystics
  (over discipline vs. output).
- **keeper_vulnerability:** *military ego* — treating the army's victory as the domain's
  purpose and soldiers as fuel, sliding toward overcharge and martyr-charges to win.
- **player_use:** the hard road to frontier access, war-resources, and decisive action;
  allies if the player values courage, antagonists if the player prizes restraint.
- **Real point:** Abyss is real, predatory, and at the gates; without the Sunbreakers'
  disciplined ferocity, Radiance's dead would already be feeding Abyss's grief-engines.
- **Dangerous flaw:** the doctrine rewards escalation — every war pushes it from
  "disciplined sacred fire" toward "maximum-output sacrifice," and its emergency powers
  never want to be returned.

### 20.4 The Forgekeeper Guild — *economic/resource*
- **short_doctrine:** "The Heart's metal is sacred work; the forge feeds the domain;
  steward the Sunstone, supply the war, profit honestly." Miners and smiths of the Heart.
- **class_base:** forge-lords, master-smiths, miners, guild-banks; a non-voting Forge-seat
  on the Council.
- **power_base:** economy (Sunstone, sacred steel, exports), the war-forges, guild-banking,
  the Relic Assay, indispensable logistics.
- **main_rival:** Kael's bloc (which wants forge output redirected to champion-devices)
  and tithe-strained labor in the Cinder Wards.
- **keeper_vulnerability:** profit-pressure toward over-mining the Heart and supplying
  overcharge components — turning sacred stewardship into Stone-stripping for margin.
- **player_use:** the economic engine; source of weapons, armor, relics, and trade;
  brokers, smugglers, and war-profiteers alike.
- **Real point:** nothing in Radiance moves without the forges — they arm the war,
  fund the rite-treasury, and steward the Heart's outer seams with real craft and pride.
- **Dangerous flaw:** the same profit-logic that builds the domain will quietly
  strip-mine the Heart and sell overcharge components if the price is right, hiding
  Stone-damage as "output gains."

### 20.5 The Ashen Martyrs — *fringe/edge*
- **short_doctrine:** "More burning is more holy. The Council fears the fire the Flame
  truly wants. The brave give everything; the cautious are cowards." A dangerous martyr-cult.
- **class_base:** the Unkindled, the shamed, the grieving, the Ash-Bound — the recruited
  desperate of the Cinder Wards.
- **power_base:** fear, fervor, the black-market in self-burn rites, a cell-network,
  the moral blackmail of shame culture.
- **main_rival:** the Dawnfire Mystics (who name them heretics) and the Ember Council.
- **keeper_vulnerability:** they are *already* the Keeper's preferred instrument inside
  Radiance — martyr-pressing, spectacle-burning, and ember-binding made into a faith.
- **player_use:** dangerous zealots, recruiters, and martyrs; a source of forbidden
  power, tragic NPCs, and the domain's darkest internal threat.

### 20.6 Kael's Vanguard (the Champion-Device Bloc) — *internal-fracture faction*
- **short_doctrine:** "Win now. The Flame gave us the means to make heroes and weapons
  that end wars fast — to refuse them is to spend more lives, not fewer. Output is mercy."
  Kael's military-research bloc pushing high-output relic / champion-device craft.

- **Dangerous flaw:** every device draws closer to the Heart and the Void; the bloc

## 22. External Domain Relations

Radiance's exploitation matrix versus all four other domains and the Void layer. Each
is an `ExternalDomainRelation` (Radiance as source_domain).

### 22.1 Radiance → Verdance
- **public_relationship:** "friendly" (trade partners, occasional uneasy allies)
- **hidden_relationship:** mutual wary respect with a deep philosophical rift — Radiance
  *burns*, Verdance *grows*; each thinks the other reckless about the soul of the world.
- **primary_conflict:** "uninvited burning" — Radiance's purge-tendency and frontier
  fires terrify Verdance, who fears Radiant flame consuming living systems and root-memory.
- **primary_dependency:** Radiance is farm-poor and *needs Verdance grain, charcoal, and
  timber*; Verdance needs Radiant steel and fire-defense against Abyss rot.
- **resource_leverage:** Verdance can starve Radiance with a grain embargo; Radiance can
  cut off sacred steel and fire-tempered tools.
- **magic_tension:** flame vs. living-cycle; Radiant purification fire can heal *or*
  destroy Verdance's wounds — Verdance distrusts the cure.
- **military_tension:** low directly; high through proxy when Radiant fire is used near
  Verdance groves.
- **historical_wound:** a past frontier fire (the Greenmourn) where a Radiant purge-burn
  destroyed a sacred grove "to deny it to Abyss" — never forgiven by the Rootwardens.
- **possible_alliance_condition:** a shared Abyss threat to both food and soul-flow;
  Radiance pledging fire-discipline near living systems.
- **possible_break_condition:** another uninvited burn, or Radiance overcharging near a grove.
- **keeper_exploit:** "stage a 'necessary' Radiant burn on Verdance soil so each blames the
  other, breaking the alliance that feeds Radiance and shields Verdance."
- **void_exploit:** whisper to Radiance that 'decay should be purged with fire' and to
  Verdance that 'corrupted growth is still natural' — corrupting both via the same incident.

### 22.2 Radiance → Tempest
- **public_relationship:** "friendly" (sky-freight partners, mutual admirers of courage)
- **hidden_relationship:** temperamental kinship and friction — both prize courage, but
  Radiance prizes *disciplined* sacrifice while Tempest prizes *unbound* freedom.
- **primary_conflict:** discipline vs. freedom — Radiance finds Tempest reckless and
  oath-loose; Tempest finds Radiance rigid and self-burning.
- **primary_dependency:** Radiance needs Tempest's Skyforge air-freight for high-value
  relic trade and rapid resupply; Tempest values Radiant sacred steel and fire-defense.
- **resource_leverage:** Tempest can ground Radiant freight and isolate frontier holds;
  Radiance can withhold sacred-steel sky-armaments.
- **magic_tension:** storm vs. fire; Tempest weather can quench or fan Radiant flame.
- **military_tension:** low; potentially high allies against Abyss (sky+fire combined arms).
- **historical_wound:** a Tempest corsair clan once abandoned a Radiant assault mid-battle
  citing 'no one has the right to bind us' — Radiance read it as dishonor.
- **possible_alliance_condition:** combined-arms war on Abyss; mutual respect for each
  other's courage; a freely-*chosen* bond (which Tempest can honor).
- **possible_break_condition:** Radiance demanding Tempest 'submit to discipline,' or
  Tempest mocking Radiant sacrifice as self-harm.
- **keeper_exploit:** "frame Radiant discipline as Tempest-style tyranny and Tempest
  freedom as Radiant-style cowardice, so neither will ally when Abyss strikes."
- **void_exploit:** tell Radiance 'bind them to the cause for their own good' (violating
  Tempest bond-law) and Tempest 'no one may make you pay any price' (rejecting all sacrifice).

### 22.3 Radiance → Sanctuary
- **public_relationship:** "ally" (the closest natural alliance — order, oath, and light)
- **hidden_relationship:** deep affinity with a quiet rivalry over *who leads the light* —
  Sanctuary's Light Eternal vs. Radiance's Eternal Flame; both claim to guard against the dark.
- **primary_conflict:** method of protection — Radiance protects by *sacrificing/burning
  the threat*; Sanctuary protects by *oath, law, and containment*. Radiance finds Sanctuary
  over-cautious; Sanctuary fears Radiant fire as uncontrollable.
- **primary_dependency:** Radiance values Sanctuary's legal/oath services and unity-defense;
  Sanctuary values Radiant fire as a weapon against Abyss undeath.
- **resource_leverage:** Sanctuary can deny legal legitimacy and refuse Radiant fire-support
  on its soil; Radiance can withhold the one weapon that truly burns Abyssian undead.
- **magic_tension:** Light Eternal vs. sacred fire — both 'good,' both potentially tyrannical
  (purity-trials meet purge-doctrine).
- **military_tension:** low; the strongest potential anti-Abyss coalition.
- **historical_wound:** Sanctuary once refused Radiant fire-support at a gate-city, fearing
  the flame would spread — the city's dead were harvested by Abyss as a result; Radiance has
  never forgotten the 'caution that fed the enemy.'
- **possible_alliance_condition:** a shared Abyss existential threat; Radiance accepting
  Sanctuary's containment law, Sanctuary accepting Radiant fire under oath-witnessed limits.
- **possible_break_condition:** a Radiant overcharge/purge on Sanctuary soil; or Sanctuary
  surveillance turning on Radiant 'fire-extremists.'
- **keeper_exploit:** "let a Radiant overcharge near Sanctuary spread the rumor 'Radiance now
  uses corrupted fire,' so Sanctuary refuses fire-support and Abyss harvests the gap."
- **void_exploit:** tell Radiance 'Sanctuary's caution is cowardice that feeds Abyss' and
  Sanctuary 'Radiance's fire is a dangerous choice you must control' — splitting the coalition.

### 22.4 Radiance → Abyss
- **public_relationship:** "war" (existential enemies)
- **hidden_relationship:** Abyss is Radiance's *predator and parasite* — it covets exactly
  what Radiance produces: martyrs, mourned dead, Phoenix souls, and grief. There is no
  reconciliation, only the hard question of *how* Radiance fights without becoming what it hates.
- **primary_conflict:** clean death-passage vs. soul-harvest — Radiance's whole religion is
  the cleanness of death; Abyss's whole economy is its theft.
- **primary_dependency:** none honest; Abyss *depends on Radiance* as a soul-resource and
  tries to keep Radiance producing fresh martyrs and false rebirths.
- **resource_leverage:** Abyss can blockade Radiant food imports to force desperation;
  Radiance's sacred fire is one of the few things that truly destroys Abyssian undeath/relics.
- **magic_tension:** sacred fire (the great counter to Abyssian soul-binding) vs. soul-harvest
  and Veilstone corruption.
- **military_tension:** open, ongoing war on the Cindergate frontier.
- **historical_wound:** the Ashfield Raids — Abyss tunnels beneath sacred burial-grounds to
  harvest the honored dead, the deepest desecration in Radiance's worldview.
- **possible_alliance_condition:** none stable; only a momentary, mutually-blackmailed
  ceasefire under a *greater* metaphysical emergency (per Chunk 1 §9 — pressure overriding hatred).
- **possible_break_condition:** N/A (already at war) — but any tolerance shatters instantly
  on a single act of soul-harvest.
- **keeper_exploit:** "make Radiance's *war* against Abyss the very thing that corrupts it —
  overcharge to win, martyr-charges to hold, forced rebirth to replace heroes — so Radiance
  scars its own Stone and soul-flow while 'defeating' Abyss, exactly as in Chunk 1 §14."
- **void_exploit:** "If Abyss burns the dead, burn hotter — pay any price to stop them." The
  Void uses the *justness* of the war to justify the overcharge that serves the Keeper.

### 22.5 Radiance → the Void Layer
- **public_relationship:** "unknown" (most Radiants do not conceive of the Void as an entity)
- **hidden_relationship:** the Void is Radiance's most intimate enemy because it wears the
  face of *deeper devotion*. It does not oppose Radiance's values; it *over-feeds* them.
- **primary_conflict:** honest, costly, consented sacrifice vs. the Void's "sacrifice without
  the inner cost" (overcharge) — the Void hollows the exact virtue Radiance is built on.
- **primary_dependency:** none — but Kael's bloc, the Ashen Martyrs, and desperate Sunbreakers
  are dangerously *susceptible*, mistaking Void-overcharge for greater faith.
- **resource_leverage:** the Void offers real, immediate power (hotter flame, won battles,
  reborn heroes); Radiance's only leverage is *discipline and rite* — the refusal to overcharge.
- **magic_tension:** the Void amplifies sacred fire while severing it from cost; the Dawnfire
  Mystics' purification is the domain's principal defense.
- **military_tension:** N/A directly; the Void escalates every Radiant warfront toward overcharge.
- **historical_wound:** the first whisper-embers in the Pyraeum after an ancestor-champion's
  'miracle' burn — the Mystics still debate whether that founding miracle was already Void-tainted.
- **possible_alliance_condition:** none — the Void cannot be allied, only resisted; any
  'use' of it is the alliance, and it always collects.
- **possible_break_condition:** N/A — the danger is precisely that Radiants don't experience
  it as an enemy to break with.
- **keeper_exploit:** the Void is the Keeper's enhancement-arm; every overcharge raises
  keeper_prison_pressure through Heart-scarring and soul-flow damage (Chunk 1 §3, §14).
- **void_exploit:** the master lie: "If your sacrifice is pure, why fear burning more?"

### 22.6 How others exploit Radiance (summary matrix)
- **Verdance exploits** Radiance's food-dependence (grain leverage) and its fear of being
  called reckless-burners.
- **Tempest exploits** Radiance's rigidity (it can out-maneuver and isolate slow-moving
  fire-armies by air) and its honor-blunt diplomacy.
- **Sanctuary exploits** Radiance's need for legal legitimacy and its anti-Abyss zeal
  (steering Radiant fire where Sanctuary law wants it).
- **Abyss exploits** Radiance's clean-death religion (harvesting its dead), its
  honor-disdain-for-spycraft (infiltration), and its food-blockade vulnerability
  (forcing overcharge desperation) — the most dangerous exploiter by far.
- **The Void exploits** Radiance's core virtue itself — selling overcharge as devotion.

## 29. Cause/Effect Examples (≥5, reaching 3rd–5th order)

Per Chunk 1 §11 and Chunk 4: **Action → Method → Witness → Interpretation → Power
Shift → Social Reaction → Systemic Effect → Metaphysical Effect → Future Pressure.**
Each chain ends with **player intervention paths** (Chunk 4 — consequences heavy but playable).

### 29.1 The Void-Amplified Victory *(the canonical Chunk 1 §14 example, expanded)*
**Root:** the player uses Void-amplified Radiance flame (overcharge) to destroy an Abyss
soul-harvest engine at the Ashfields before it activates. *Depth: 5.*
- **1st (immediate):** engine destroyed; captured souls saved; Radiant soldiers cheer.
  Radiance reputation +15; Abyss op disrupted (control −10); captives trust the player;
  **void_resonance +4**; a faint whisper-ember lingers where the player burned.
- **2nd (scene/session):** Ashen Martyrs praise "burning past safe limits"; Dawnfire
  Mystics are alarmed (suspicion +10) — they saw fire that bypassed cost; Forgekeepers
  ask whether it can be replicated.
- **3rd (local/system):** doctrine pressure shifts toward high-output relic use; young
  acolytes volunteer for dangerous rites; an Abyss spy seeds the rumor "Radiance now uses
  corrupted fire"; the Cindergate burn-fields gain new ember-haunts.
- **4th (regional/political):** Kael's bloc cites the victory to push champion-device
  doctrine; Sanctuary hesitates to accept Radiant fire-support; Verdance fears uninvited
  burning; Radiance polarizes between victory and purity (the Output Schism intensifies).
- **5th (mythic/metaphysical):** the Keeper benefits — a heroic victory created
  **void_dependency, martyr pressure, interdomain mistrust, Sunstone Heart-scarring, and
  future Stone stress**; architect_sacrifice_integrity dips; keeper_prison_pressure rises.
- **VERDAX explanation:** the action was heroic but not clean. Radiance honored the courage;
  the Void exploited the method; the Keeper benefits if Radiance confuses spectacle with
  sacred sacrifice.
- **Intervention:** publicly acknowledge the danger; submit the relic/flame for Mystic
  purification; credit the captives, not the spectacle; help the Mystics define safe limits;
  expose and burn out the Void residue before it is replicated.

### 29.2 The Forced Rebirth of a Champion
**Root:** Captain-Champion Renn dies in an overcharge accident at Cindergate; a grieving,
desperate command forces a Phoenix rebirth without true death-consent to keep the army's
morale. The player can perform, refuse, or expose the rite. *Depth: 5.*
- **1st:** a body that looks like Renn lives; the army rallies; morale +major *now*.
- **2nd:** the Reborn does not fully recognize his comrades; Liora Emberkin (who would have
  blessed a true rite) refuses to bless this one; Mystic-Mother Seraphel demands an inquiry.
- **3rd:** a **false_rebirth** soul-wound forms; rumor splits — "a miracle returned our
  champion" vs. "command desecrated the rite"; the Output Schism gains a martyr-symbol.
- **4th:** the Council must choose between disavowing the rite (admitting the desecration and
  losing the morale boost) or covering it up (deepening institutional dishonesty); Kael's
  bloc is tempted to make hollow-Reborn champions standard practice.
- **5th:** if normalized, mass forced rebirth corrupts soul flow's most sacred exception —
  the founding act inverted; ember-haunts and hollow Reborn proliferate; keeper_prison_pressure
  rises sharply (the Keeper's preferred Radiance outcome).
- **Intervention:** perform a merciful Ashening to *re-death* the hollow Reborn; expose the
  forced rite and submit to Mystic judgment; comfort the grieving command toward a true,
  consented memorial; prevent the bloc from making it doctrine.

### 29.3 The Shamed Boy and the Martyr Cult
**Root:** the player publicly shames the un-scarred boy Tomas as a coward to motivate a
frightened militia in the Cinder Wards. *Depth: 3 (escalating toward 4).*
- **1st:** the militia steadies for the moment; Tomas is humiliated; Hearth-Warden Mira's
  trust in the player drops (suspicion +8).
- **2nd:** Tomas, ashamed, seeks worth where it's offered — the Ashen Martyr recruiter Brand
  welcomes him; a small rumor spreads that "even the outsider says the un-scarred are cowards."
- **3rd:** Ashen Martyr recruitment in the ward ticks up; the Shame Harvest power-struggle
  intensifies; Mira's reform efforts are undercut; civilian_morale in the Cinder Wards −meaningful.
- **4th:** if Tomas commits to a spectacle-burn, a martyr-wave can follow; the ward tips toward
  the Martyrs and Abyss debt-brokers; a depth-4 social fracture in the lower caste.
- **5th (if ignored):** the Cinder Wards become a self-sustaining martyr-engine feeding soul-scars
  and ember-haunts — exactly the shame-culture exploit the Keeper wants.
- **Intervention:** publicly retract and credit Tomas's quiet courage; break Brand's recruiting
  pipeline; back Mira's honor-reform; give Tomas an honest path to standing that costs *him*
  something real but survivable.

### 29.4 The Hidden Heart-Scarring Exposed
**Root:** the player discovers the Ember Council is hiding the Sunstone Heart's scarring as
"flame fatigue" and must decide what to do with the truth. *Depth: 4 (touching 5).*
- **1st:** the player holds a dangerous secret; the Council (via Vaelorin) becomes wary if it
  suspects the player knows.
- **2nd (expose honestly):** the Mystics' respect for the player rises (truth about sacrifice is
  holy); the Council's fear/suspicion rises (its legitimacy rests on the secret); a public shock.
- **3rd:** rumor mutates — "the Flame is dying" / "the Council hid the death of the Flame";
  every faction blames the others (the Output Schism and the Levy struggle both flare); panic
  ripples through the Dawnlighting faithful.
- **4th:** Radiance must publicly confront *why* the Heart scarred — forcing a reckoning over
  champion-devices and overcharge; either real reform (re-cooling the Heart via honest sacrifice)
  or a deeper cover-up and a legitimacy crisis.
- **5th:** honest reckoning + a season of honest sacrifice *raises* domain_stone_stability and
  reinforces the prison; a deeper cover-up *lowers* it and hands the Keeper a domain that lies
  about its own sacred fire.
- **Intervention:** broker a controlled disclosure with a remedy already in hand (Mystic
  re-consecration plan); protect Vaelorin's ability to reform rather than humiliating him into
  rigidity; rally honest sacrifice across the domain to re-cool the Heart; expose the suppressed
  overcharge log as the cause.

### 29.5 The Grain Embargo Desperation *(economic → metaphysical)*
**Root:** Abyss (via raiders and proxy pressure) blockades Radiance's food imports during the
Cindergate campaign; the domain begins to starve, and command considers Heart-tapping for a
war-winning burn. *Depth: 4 (escalating to 5).*
- **1st:** food prices spike; the Cinder Wards hunger first; civilian_morale and resource_security drop.
- **2nd:** Verdance is asked for emergency grain but hesitates (the old Greenmourn wound and fear
  of uninvited burning); Forgekeepers push to strip-mine the Heart's inner seams "for the war effort."
- **3rd:** Sunbreaker hardliners and Kael's bloc argue for a Heart-tap overcharge to end the
  Cindergate war fast and break the blockade; the Mystics resist; the Levy struggle flares.
- **4th:** if Radiance Heart-taps, it wins the battle but scars the Heart and proves the overcharge
  shortcut at *strategic* scale; Verdance and Sanctuary grow more wary of Radiant fire; the food
  crisis becomes a doctrine crisis.
- **5th:** the Keeper converts an economic siege into Stone-scarring and soul-flow damage — Radiance
  defeats the blockade by becoming more corruptible; keeper_prison_pressure rises.
- **Intervention:** secure an honest Verdance grain alliance (pledging fire-discipline near living
  systems, healing the Greenmourn wound); break the blockade with Tempest sky-freight and disciplined
  arms instead of overcharge; ease the Cinder Ward famine to starve the Martyrs of recruits; refuse
  the Heart-tap and pay an honest, witnessed military cost instead.

### 29.6 The Liora Death and the Renaming *(NPC death → social/metaphysical)*
**Root:** Liora Emberkin (Dawnward battle-priest and possible romance) dies holding a medical tent
open during the ashfall at Cindergate so the wounded can be evacuated, while the player is committed
elsewhere. *Depth: 4 (with depth-5 if soul-flow is mishandled).*
- **1st:** Liora dies a clean, willing sacrifice; the wounded are saved; grief sweeps the garrison.
- **2nd:** soldiers begin to rename the player according to the choice that led there (e.g.,
  "The One Who Was Elsewhere" or, if the player's order sent her, harsher); healing capacity at
  Cindergate drops; a forge-smith refuses further work in protest.
- **3rd:** Mystic-Mother Seraphel demands an inquiry into how the Dawnward were deployed; a rumor
  begins that "command spends healers like arrows"; the Levy struggle flares (was she spent as fuel?).
- **4th:** if the inquiry finds soldiers were spent without shared risk, the military-ego corruption
  rises and trust in command drops; if Liora is honored as a true sacrifice and her killers (the
  ashfall conditions / Abyss) named, the garrison's resolve hardens honorably instead.
- **5th (if her body/soul is mishandled):** if grief drives a *forced rebirth* of Liora, a depth-5
  false_rebirth catastrophe follows; if she is given a clean Ashening, her sacrifice *strengthens*
  soul flow and the prison.
- **Intervention:** ensure Liora a clean, witnessed Ashening; refuse any forced rebirth no matter the
  grief; submit honestly to Seraphel's inquiry; share the cost publicly (don't let command scapegoat
  the dead); let her name be kept truthfully so the renaming honors rather than condemns.

## 30. Open Questions

1. **The first miracle.** Was the founding ancestor-champion's legendary "miracle burn" — the
   one that birthed the first whisper-embers — already Void-tainted? Is Radiance's heroic origin
   partly a corruption the Mystics have spent centuries denying?
2. **Phoenix limits.** Exactly how rare is true Phoenix rebirth — once a generation? Can the *same*
   soul be reborn twice? Does each true rebirth cost the Sunstone Heart a measure of stability, making
   even sanctioned rebirth a slow drain?
3. **Heart depletion.** Is the Sunstone Heart finite? If over-mined or over-tapped, does the Eternal
   Flame eventually gutter — and is *that* the true "flame fatigue" the Council fears?
4. **Kael's arc.** Is Kael redeemable, or is he fated to become the corruption vessel? Can the player
   keep him honest, or does the domain need him to fall to learn the lesson?
5. **Architect identity.** Which Architect carried the principle of sacrifice/renewal, and is an echo
   of them dormant within the Eternal Flame? Could the player commune with it?
6. **Clean rebirth vs. Abyss.** Is Phoenix rebirth metaphysically related to Abyss's false rebirth —
   the same soul-operation done with consent vs. without? Could Abyss learn to *counterfeit* Phoenix
   rites, and could the Mystics tell the difference?
7. **Shame reform.** Can the sacrifice-meritocracy be reformed to credit quiet sacrifice without
   collapsing the honor-system that holds the domain together?
8. **Inter-domain fire.** Can Radiant purification fire safely *heal* Verdance rot or Abyss soul-binding,
   or does using it outside Radiance always risk uninvited burning? (Ties to the Greenmourn wound.)

## 31. Locked Decisions

1. **Radiance is a sacred-fire military civilization** whose core belief is that *true power must
   be paid for through willing sacrifice.* It is a civilization (government, economy, class, military,
   religion, daily life), not a theme.
2. **True virtues:** sacrifice, renewal, courage, sacred fire, honor, rebirth, discipline.
3. **Corrupted forms:** hubris, martyr addiction, purge doctrine, forced rebirth, hero spectacle,
   military ego, shame culture.
4. **The Keeper exploit is fixed:** confuse sacrifice with glory — "If I am willing to die, whatever
   I do must be holy." The Keeper corrupts by *overcommitment*, sliding willing sacrifice toward
   coerced, spectacle, spent-from-others, purge, and forced rebirth.
5. **The Void lie is fixed:** "If your sacrifice is pure, why fear burning more?" The Void offers
   overcharge — real power that bypasses the inner cost, deepening void_dependency and Heart-scarring.
6. **The domain Stone source is the Eternal Flame, anchored in the Sunstone Heart** — a flame-form
   fragment of the fractured pure Nexus Stone, and a load-bearing piece of the Keeper's prison. Its
   stability tracks the *honesty* of Radiance's sacrifices, not their quantity.
7. **The Phoenix and Phoenix rebirth are real but a narrow exception** to soul flow — requiring true
   death, consent, sanctioned rite, and an accepting Flame. Faked or forced, the result is the
   dangerous soul-wound **false_rebirth.**
8. **Ruling authority is the Ember Council; spiritual/magical authority is the Dawnfire Mystics;
   military doctrine is the Sunbreaker War Doctrine; the economic/forge power is the Forgekeepers;
   the fringe is the Ashen Martyrs; the central internal fracture is Kael's champion-device bloc.**
9. **Kael is a sincere hero, not a cackling villain** — the corruption vessel through whom hubris,
   overcharge, and forced rebirth could capture Radiance. "Output is mercy" is a defensible reading
   of Radiance, which is exactly why it is dangerous.
10. **Radiance is forge-rich and farm-poor** — import-dependent for food, which is its central
    economic vulnerability and a primary Abyss/Keeper attack vector.
11. **Abyss is Radiance's existential enemy and parasite**, coveting its martyrs, mourned dead, and
    Phoenix souls; the gravest danger is that the *just war* against Abyss is the very thing that
    corrupts Radiance (overcharge, martyr-charges, forced rebirth).
12. **Sanctuary is Radiance's closest natural ally; Verdance and Tempest are friendly-but-wary
    trade partners**; the Void is an intimate enemy wearing the face of deeper devotion.
13. **Each faction has a real point and a dangerous flaw**, and relationships are fluid: enemies can
    be tolerated under metaphysical emergency, allies can fracture over method (overcharge, forced
    rebirth, denied Ashening).
14. **Lying about a sacrifice is the deepest blasphemy**; accusations of fake or Void-tainted fire are
    the sharpest social weapons in the domain.
15. **All consequences obey the Chunk 4 causality formula and depth rules:** soul-flow/Void/Keeper/
    Stone/identity/forced-rebirth events are depth 5; consequences are heavy but playable, always
    explained in-world, and never random.
